Hey! That’s an awesome project—you can definitely draw a lot of inspiration from Cyberpunk 2077, but when it comes to slang and phrases, there are a few things to keep in mind to avoid legal trouble:

What’s Safe to Use (Generally OK):
General Cyberpunk Tropes & Language:

Common words like “chrome,” “net,” “deck,” “runner,” “ice,” “boost,” “choom,” etc., are not unique to Cyberpunk 2077. Many originated from older cyberpunk media like Neuromancer, Blade Runner, or the original Cyberpunk 2020 TTRPG. These are considered part of the genre.

Common Slang:

Words like:

Choom – slang for "friend" (also used in Cyberpunk 2020)

Preem – short for “premium” (meaning great/cool)

Nova – amazing, badass

Eddies – slang for currency (Eurodollars)

Chromehead – someone who’s overly cybered-up

Input/Output – slang for girlfriend/boyfriend

Many of these are from Cyberpunk 2020, which predates 2077. Mike Pondsmith’s R. Talsorian Games owns those terms, not CD Projekt Red.

If you're inspired by these, try putting your own spin on them to keep your world distinct.

What to Avoid or Tweak (Legally Risky or Confusing):
Proprietary Names/Terms:

Night City, Arasaka, Militech, Relic, V, Johnny Silverhand, Netrunner (as a class name) – these are specific to CD Projekt Red and/or R. Talsorian Games. Using them as-is could lead to copyright or trademark issues.

Lore-Specific Slang:

If it was created specifically for the Cyberpunk 2077 game and doesn’t appear in earlier media (like Cyberpunk 2020), it’s better to avoid or remix it.

UI/Visual Phrases:

Terms like “Braindance”, “Cyberpsychosis” (as used specifically in 2077) have specific mechanical and visual identity in the game. Again, remix or rename if you're using similar concepts.

Tips for Making Your Own Slang:
Build from Culture & Setting:

Create slang that reflects your world’s economy, tech, oppression, street culture. For example, if your world is heavily corporate-owned, a slur for corporate workers might be “suits” or “cordogs.”

Play with Word Shortening & Fusion:

“Neural Interface” becomes “neuro.”

“Augmented freak” might become “aufreak.”

Regionalize it:

Give different slang to various factions, cities, or levels of society. This makes your setting feel alive and unique.

If You Plan to Publish Commercially:
Stick to original terms and genre tropes rather than direct lifts.

Consider using slang inspired by Cyberpunk 2077, but changed enough that it clearly belongs to your own world.
