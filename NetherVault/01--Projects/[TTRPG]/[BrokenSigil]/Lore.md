# Broken Sigil — Lore

> Mythic Fantasy — Medieval dark fantasy with cosmic horror roots

---

## Core Cosmological Truth

The setting *appears* as regular medieval dark fantasy. The underlying structure hides cosmic horror roots:

- **Gods don't exist** as divine entities — what people pray to are Eldritch entities with very limited influence on the plane
- **Magic comes from the decaying body** of a cosmic entity
- **Most monsters are corrupted animals** — singular, unique, legendary

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The horror isn't loud, it's **structural**. It's not that a tentacle god attacks your village — it's that the entire framework of the world (gods, magic, monsters) are symptoms of something broken, vast, and indifferent.

---

## The Cosmic Entity

A vast, decaying cosmic being whose body permeates the world. Its decay is the source of all arcane magic — mana.

- Magic use is inherently tied to entropy and corruption
- Sorcerers are essentially parasites on a corpse, and the corpse is *finite*
- As the body decays further, magic becomes more available but more unstable and corrupting
- The world has a quiet apocalyptic undertone even without anyone knowing it

### The Slow Apocalypse

If the cosmic entity continues to decay, even intentional magic will become too entropic for silver to channel. The most advanced magical civilization in history will find that all their finest tools simply *stop working* — not because the tools broke, but because the magic itself became too corrupted to be shaped.

---

## The Ancients — The Race Before

A civilization that existed before the collision of the cosmic being.

### Golden & Silver Blood

The surviving ancients may have gold or silver in their blood, granting immunity to corruption — they are simply **incompatible** with it, the same way gold coins are.

**Golden blood:**
- Vanishingly rare, possibly singular — one or two in the entire world
- Incredibly old, possibly not recognized for what they are
- Age strangely or not at all
- Magic behaves oddly around them
- Survived by being *quiet*

**Silver blood:**
- Slightly more common — a scattered, diluted bloodline
- Most carriers don't know they have it
- Unusually resistant to certain corruptions
- Wounds from eldritch creatures heal cleaner
- Silver doesn't tarnish when they wear it
- Folk traditions about "blessed" family lines are actually genetic memory of this heritage

---

## Gold & Silver — The Deeper Truth

Gold and silver aren't just currency metals — they are **remnants of something older than the cosmic entity**. They predate the current cosmic order entirely. They're **scar tissue from a previous universe**, or fragments of a dead god that predates even the Eldritch beings.

The corruption slides off them the way water slides off oil — they're simply not made of compatible substance.

### What People Believe vs. What's True

| Surface Understanding | Deep Truth |
| --------------------- | ---------- |
| Gold is "blessed" or "pure" | Gold is from *before* — incompatible with the current cosmic order |
| Gold veins are geological formations | Gold veins are **wounds** in the earth — places where something impossibly ancient bled through |
| Silvered weapons exploit a monster weakness | Silver physically disrupts eldritch energy — it refuses to cooperate with what the creatures *are* |
| Gold can't be forged because it's precious | Gold can't be forged because magic literally cannot touch it |

### The Paradox of Channeling

Silver and gold distinguish between **structured and unstructured** energy:
- Eldritch corruption = mana in decay, entropic, formless → **rejected**
- Shaped mana, cast with intention and precision → **channeled cleanly**

This is only possible because mana still has *some* structure left. The corruption hasn't fully won. **Yet.**

## The Golden Temples

Ancient underground chambers built with golden walls. The ancients didn't build them to store wealth — they built them as **shelters**, emergency refuges when they realized the collision was coming.

### Properties

- **Dead zones for corruption** — nothing eldritch can enter, sustain itself, or even perceive what's inside
- Magic cannot enter — no summoning, scrying, or magical tunneling
- For the ancients, probably everyday structures — hospitals, libraries, places to think clearly
- For current civilization, the most precious real estate in existence

### Political Consequences

- Kingdoms control golden temples as treasuries — they don't just have money, they have **a place where reality is guaranteed**
- Attacking magically is impossible — any siege must be entirely physical
- Whoever controls the temple controls legitimacy
- The temples are **older than the kingdoms built above them** — every kingdom is parasitic on ancient infrastructure they didn't build and don't understand
- There are almost certainly doors that no one has opened, corridors that haven't been mapped, sections sealed for forgotten reasons

### The Deepest Horror

Somewhere in the deepest, oldest chambers: records of what the ancients were hiding from — written in a language no one alive reads, on walls that magic cannot touch, waiting for someone to translate them.

---

## Monsters — Unique & Singular

There are no *medusas* — there is **The Medusa**, a singular monster with renown and power. Every major monster is a mythological figure, not a species.

- Monsters are **corrupted animals** — proximity to cosmic decay or Eldritch influence
- Encountering one is an *event*, not a combat encounter
- Players hear whispers about *The Basilisk* weeks before they might meet it
- There is tragedy underneath the horror — *was she always this? Can it spread?*

## The "Gods" — Eldritch Entities by Archetype

None of these are actually gods. They are Eldritch entities that people have *interpreted* through human archetypes. The myths aren't wrong — they describe real phenomena, just written in the wrong language.

| Archetype | Human Interpretation | Eldritch Reality |
| --------- | -------------------- | ---------------- |
| **Triple Goddesses / Fate** | Three aspects knowing past, present, future | An entity existing across multiple points in time simultaneously; three aspects are how mortal minds cope with something that is *not singular* |
| **Psychopomps** | Compassionate guides of the dead | Something drawn to death the way rot draws flies — a scavenger, not a shepherd |
| **Primordial Gods** | Creators of the world | The cosmic entity itself — the only essentially *true* myths, universally misunderstood as creation when it was really *collapse* |
| **Celestial Gods** | Rulers of sun and sky | Entities at the boundary of the plane, partially outside it; influence leaks in like light through cracks |
| **Culture Heroes** | Defiant gift-givers (fire, language) | Gifts were real, but the motive was never what myths claimed — spreading the gift served the entity's own incomprehensible purpose |
| **Death Gods** | Guardians of the afterlife | Possibly no entity at all — the "death god" myths are what cultures invented to cope with the fact that *nothing watches over that threshold* |
| **Cyclical / Liminal** | Gods of seasons and thresholds | The edges of the cosmic body — decay creating rhythmic effects; mortals built gods around a heartbeat |
| **Personifications / Daimons** | Embodiments of concepts | Not true entities — emergent phenomena, concentrations of human experience that attract minor Eldritch attention |
| **Rustic / Nature Gods** | Guardians of the wild | The corruption itself — the slow spread of cosmic decay through the natural world given a face |
| **Craft / Civilization Gods** | Patrons of cities and craft | Entities that find structured human society *useful* — organized worship is a more reliable signal than chaotic prayer |
| **Tutelary / Guardian Gods** | River gods, forest spirits | Residual fragments of cosmic influence pooled in geographical locations — occasionally responsive |

### The Key Insight

> Prayers might even get answered — not out of love or divine will, but because the entity on the other end occasionally stirs and its influence leaks through. The priest isn't deluded — he's just feeding something he can't comprehend.

## The Two Magical Traditions

### Arcane Magic

- Source: mana from the decaying cosmic entity
- Materials: disrupted by gold and silver
- Practitioners: academic mages, wizards, sorcerers
- Truth: sorcerers are parasites on a cosmic corpse
- Control: golden manacles prevent casting

### Animism

- Source: something that was here *before* the entity arrived
- Materials: wood (holds memory) and iron (carries intention)
- Practitioners: shamans, rural traditions, marginalized communities
- Truth: animist spirits **survived** the cosmic collision — they predate it and will outlast it
- Control: **golden manacles have no effect** — the state doesn't know this

> [!CAUTION]
> A player who discovers that golden manacles don't work on animists has found something **politically explosive**. The state's ultimate tool of control has a blind spot it doesn't know about.

### Ancient Animist Knowledge

Animist traditions may contain fragmented knowledge of the world *before* the collision — not as history, but encoded in ritual, in specific words, in gestures that predate any living civilization. Not understood as history. Just done, the way it has always been done, because that's how you show the old ones respect.

## Campaign Tiers

### Adventure Campaigns

- Focus on monster hunting, exploration, survival
- The world reads as dark fantasy with singular legendary creatures
- Fully satisfying without the cosmic horror layer
- "Hunt The Basilisk, survive the cursed forest, get paid"

### Truth-Seeking Campaigns

- Emerge organically when curious players start pulling threads
- The cursed forest isn't cursed by a witch — it's growing over a site where the cosmic body is closest to the surface
- The Basilisk isn't just a monster — it's a concentration of decay, and killing it might *do something* to the local magic field
- Everything adventure-tier players walked past becomes a clue

> The genre shift happens without anyone announcing it. A curious player just starts pulling threads — *why does magic feel different near the old ruins, why do all corrupted creatures drift toward the same region, why does the god's voice sound like it's coming from very far away* — and suddenly they're in a different game.

## Threads for Curious Players

Reliable, discoverable seams in the world — things that seem like flavor but are actually load-bearing lore:

1. The smell/taste of magic
2. How corrupted animals behave near certain sites
3. Inconsistencies in religious texts nobody questions
4. Ruins older than recorded civilization
5. The isolation of Forbidden Knowledge on the character sheet
6. Why gold can't be touched by magic
7. Why some family lines are called "blessed"
8. Dreams near old sites
9. Places where sound behaves oddly
10. Prayers answered in ways that feel mechanical rather than loving
