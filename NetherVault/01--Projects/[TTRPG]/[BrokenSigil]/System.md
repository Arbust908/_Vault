### ─

## Heroic Abilities

### Weapon Training

| Ability          | Requirement         | Effect                  |
| ---------------- | ------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Weapon Training  | 60 in one weapon    | Base of 40 on all weapons |
| Weapon Mastery   | 80 in one weapon    | Base of 55 on all weapons |

> The journey is *swordsman → warrior*. You can't shortcut to versatility — you must earn mastery first. When a player hits 60, all those zeroes become 40s — a satisfying visual and narrative beat.
>
> A mage never gets there unless they sacrifice the skill investment that would make them a good mage. The tradeoff enforces identity at every level.

## Forbidden Knowledge — Mechanics (GM Eyes Only)

### How to Earn It

Points are **never** earned through study or training. They come from **witnessing things that shouldn't exist:**

- Surviving direct contact with one of the old beings
- Reading a text that predates human civilization
- Successfully interpreting a dream sent by something vast
- Watching someone else's mind break from the truth and *not breaking yourself*

Each point earned should cost something — a failed Sanity check, a permanent memory, a detail the character can never un-know.

### How It Works — Hybrid Model

- **Passive revelation** up to a threshold: the GM feeds the player more true information about what's really happening. FK 30 → they notice the gods' iconography is *wrong*. FK 60 → they can read the original language carved beneath every temple.
- **Active but cursed** past the threshold: the GM calls for secret, blind rolls in situations most characters don't even recognize as *situations*. Success means they understood something they weren't meant to.

## Magic System

### Two Supernatural Substrates

**The Arcane Layer**
- Mana originates from the decaying cosmic entity
- Powerful but increasingly corrupted
- Works through structured channeling
- Disrupted by gold and silver

**The Animist Layer**
- Something that was here *before* the entity arrived
- Diffuse and ancient
- Carried in living wood and worked iron
- Completely indifferent to gold and silver — it was never part of that system

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Golden manacles **do nothing** to an animist. The state's ultimate tool of control over magical practitioners has a blind spot it doesn't know about.

### Metal Properties — Anti-Magic & Channeling

Silver and gold don't distinguish between "magic" and "anti-magic" — they distinguish between **structured and unstructured** energy.

- **Anti-magic use:** Raw silver/gold repels eldritch corruption (entropic, formless mana)
- **Channeling use:** In the hands of a skilled craftsman, silver/gold becomes a *perfect container* for shaped mana — it refuses to absorb or distort the energy passing through it

> Common enchanted items (iron, wood) create interference and degrade. Silver-channeled enchantments are *clean* — what goes in comes out exactly as intended.

### Craftsmanship Tiers

| Material                  | Properties                                                    |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Iron/Steel weapons        | Standard, affected by magic, degrade against eldritch creatures |
| Silvered weapons          | Disrupt corruption, hold integrity against eldritch contact    |
| Silver-channeled items    | Elite equipment, precise and durable enchantments              |
| Ancient gold-channeled artifacts | Unique relics, principles no one fully understands        |

### Golden Manacles

Thick golden manacles prevent arcane mages from casting. Likely a steel core with gold inlay in specific patterns. Tradition keeps them heavy and obvious — partly practical, partly a statement.

### Animist Materials

Animists use **wood and iron** for totems and fetishes:
- Wood holds memory
- Iron carries weight and intention
- These are material properties, not metaphors

## Economy

### Currency Ratios

**1 Gold = 1,000 Silver** (no copper mentioned yet—to be decided)

| Currency | Domain                | Who Uses It               |
| -------- | -------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Copper   | Daily life — meals, candles, bad inns | Common people       |
| Silver   | Skilled labor, weapons, travel, a soldier's weekly wage | Merchant class |
| Gold     | Ships, land titles, castle repairs, political bribes, ancient artifacts | Governments, noble houses |

> A single gold coin appearing in a street transaction is *suspicious*. A farmer might die never having held one.

### Why Gold & Silver Are Currency

Gold and silver are **immune to magical manipulation** — no spell can create them, no transmutation can fake them, no illusion lasts when pressed to a coin. In a world saturated with magic, that property makes them the only trustworthy medium of exchange.

### Economic Consequences

- **Forgery is impossible** → trade is unusually trustworthy, but class stratification is rigid and ancient
- **Powerful mages are relatively poor** unless they inherit or take wealth by force — a court wizard cannot conjure their salary
- **Gold mines are strategically vital** beyond normal resources — wars are fought over them, quietly

## Product Line

| Product        | Audience                   | Horror Layer                              |
| -------------- | -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Quickstart     | New player groups          | Surface only + subtle off-notes           |
| System Book    | Players & GMs              | Mostly surface, early seeds               |
| Setting Book   | GMs & curious players      | Richer seeds, some overlap with System    |
| Secrets Book   | GMs (+ curious players for crunch) | Full reveal                        |

### Secrets Book — Design Principle

All mechanics, monsters, NPCs, and adventures in the Secrets Book are tied to the *truth* of the world. Spells are tied to the true nature of mana. NPCs who were presented as "crazy" in the Setting Book are reframed as *knowers*. Adventures build on Setting Book threads — surface resolutions reveal they only addressed symptoms.

### Seed Taxonomy (Consistent Across All Products)

1. **Behavioral wrongness** — animals reacting strangely, prayers answered in mechanical rather than loving ways
2. **Historical gaps** — ruins older than recorded civilization, contradictions in religious texts nobody questions
3. **Sensory wrongness** — magic having a smell or taste, places making sound behave oddly, dreams near old sites
