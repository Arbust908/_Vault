## Introduction
**Flow Comes First.** Let's play a game where things get done.
* We'll keep rule arguments to a minimum, stick to rulings where possible.
* If you have a hard time with focus, try keeping notes.
* Have your dice out, your character sheet available.

**Immersion is King.** Let's play a game where people are immersed.
* Respect the scene. Keep meta talk to a minimum.
* Respect the characters. Don't joke when people are roleplaying.
* Respect the ignorance. Artless exposition might be worse than focused ignorance.
* Respect the player. Players can ask for what they need, don't volunteer it unprompted.

## The Long Rest

They need to spend three short rests that aren't interrupted with danger, class features, or moving about too much. Or rather, that's two whole days doing nothing. A solid weekend. So! It's downtime! They'll want to craft, build, organize themselves in all manner of ways. One thing they could learn here is how a better commissioner could turn things around.

#### Construction
A few projects are pulling at Church and the Bridgemen at the moment.

* The well is 1d4-1 weeks from being complete. If they can get it done in a week, it's essentially free thanks to the church. Otherwise the cost is N18.000 notes a week to import 3 tons of stone required for a deeper well from Brooksford.
* The tavern is 4 days of work from being finished, provided it's got three laborers working on it. That could be the bridgemen, or someone hired. Hiring costs N20 a day per laborer.

The Guildhall is a bit more of a complicated situation, let's dive in.

* In raw materials, at market price, the guildhall costs about N500.000. 
	* If it has to be imported, it could cost as much as double the market rate.
	* If sourced locally, it would cost triple the market rate and the time needed with the small labor force would lengthen construction by 1 additional die roll.
	* But that would count as a private lumber company providing additional N200 of AWI income.
* Construction time varies wildly depending on how much they invest in labor.
	* At a skeleton crew of only the Bridgemen, this might take a year. (4d6 months)
	* With a fully staffed team of a hundred laborers, it could be done in 3d6 weeks. But that would cost N10.000 a week (max N240.000). 
	* However, it would increase local average income by N200 due to the presence of private corporate funding.
* Either buying locally or hiring local crew removes stagnation while in effect.
	* And remember, unemployment and the tax exemption are inextricably tied to AWI.

Ultimately, a big truth here is the fact that Crossroads could be a huge boon to the local economy. It would begin to curb the collapse of the town. It would push bankruptcy from 5 weeks away to 7 weeks. Which doesn't solve everything, but it's a *good start!*

The realization here for the PCs is that this community's frozen and that'll break it. Moving the money around creates wealth!

###### Why Is Local Production So Ineffective?
This is a small community town with little in the way of private enterprise. As such all the locals kind of do everything. They make their own clothes, candles, oil, soap, houses, roads, guns, tools, fences, food, and timber.

If they were to stop doing all that, they'd have to import more than normal, so they'd have to be paid enough to compensate for their lost local production. Making everything by yourself is inefficient, so in terms of excess, there is none.

Which is to say, paying that extra would help get this community rolling by moving the economy, but it won't make the guildhall construction as quickly as possible.

#### Local Politics
Tranter could suggest to Barton that a good relationship with local politics may be the key to winning this election. They've already got a good start, but one wing to approach is to help sort local problems and promising to extrapolate on a grander scale.

Brooke thinks highly of Seraph's mayoral skills. Even if she's kind of disgusted by him at the moment.

> "You oversaw a massive boom in Gunlock from your first day in office."

Claire Anders, former mayor of Redford, wants to sit down with Seraph, Barton, and Brooke to talk about how best to improve the community, 

> "Businesses are the heart of a modern community. And this one's is a tale as old as time, merely told at the extreme end of the spectrum."
> 
> "Nobody's willing to invest because there's no infrastructure, and there's no infrastructure since nobody's willing to invest. It's the chicken and the egg."

She suggests the following steps:

* Get the economy moving, inject cash.
	* Involve corporations, either by enticing them or founding them.
	* Crossroads is a good idea, if they can get it to work. If they invest.
	* Invest in the locals, their ambitions, their crazy schemes.
* Improve development! If this county were at 100% development, with nothing else changed, bankruptcy would be 3 nearly 3 years away.
* Increase population is important but at the moment it would cripple the county.
	* Doubling pops now double the rate of loss.
	* If average weekly income, development are improved, or features are built. It would make this area really wealthy. 
* Once the economy moves slightly, higher taxes will lift them out of debt.
	* Claire and Brooke have crunched the numbers and they believe a 14% tax rate on a Loggers County that builds would delay bankruptcy to 28 weeks. Even still...
* A bridge is good, but a permanent ferryman in both towns must be hired in the interim. Or a rope-barge.
