## Introduction

Arcane Trickster Rogues can learn and cast a few spells from the Wizard’s spell list, but your limited number of spells known and spell slots means that you need to be very economical with your spellcasting. Pick spells carefully, and try to get a lot of value out of each spell.

The need to split your focus between Dexterity and Intelligence can make the Rogue somewhat MAD (Multiple Ability Dependent). However, the presence of the Cantrip True Strike allows you to focus on Intelligence, which supports your spellcasting at the cost of anything related to Dexterity, including your AC. The advice in this article will attempt to cater to both Dexterity-focused and Intelligence-focused builds, but be sure to read the individual spell assessments rather than blindly picking blue-rated options.

Arcane Tricksters can only replace a single prepared spell each time that you gain a Rogue level, and you can prepare a maximum of 13 spells at level 20. This means that choosing effective spells is crucial, especially late in your career when you’ll have little time to correct a poor choice.

This article primarily caters to Arcane Trickster Rogues, but other martial characters with the Magic Initiate feat may also find it helpful.

## Rogues and Attack Spells

You cannot deliver Sneak Attack with a Spell Attack. This means that Cantrips like Fire Bolt don’t work, but spells which call for a Weapon Attack, like Booming Blade and True Strike, all work.

Gishes (characters that mix spellcasting and martial capabilities) frequently need to choose between using attack Cantrips or using Extra Attack, and the math there can become complicated. However, Rogues don’t get Extra Attack. Unless you’re relying on fighting with two weapons, using a Cantrip is an easy boost to your attacks.

Because Rogue’s damage output comes primarily from Sneak Attack, missing a single attack can be devastating. Rolling a second d20, either from Advantage or from a second attack roll, boosts your average damage per round (DPR) by as much as 50%. Using a Cantrip means that you can’t use a Nick weapon to make a second attack during your Attack action. Instead, consider Vex weapons like hand crossbows and rapiers, and use stealth or Steady Aim to get Advantage on your first attack against a new target.

## Arcane Trickster Rogue Spells

### Arcane Trickster Rogue Cantrips

-   Acid Splash ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Rogues are at their best when they’re focused on dealing big damage to single targets. Acid Splash won’t help you with that. It’s tempting to add area damage to your options, but the gap in how much damage Acid Splash does compared to Sneak Attack is massive, especially when you consider other things like Cunning Strike and Weapon Mastery.
-   Blade Ward ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Excellent for any melee build, but not worth the Action to cast it in combat. If you can cast this before walking into a fight, it’s fantastic.
-   Booming Blade ([SCAG / TCoE](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Attack, then use Cunning Action to Disengage. Instant hit-and-run tactics, and enemies take extra damage if they chase you.
-   Chill Touch ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Spell attacks won’t help you.
-   Control Flames[](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/): Bad.
-   Create Bonfire ([EEPC / XGtE](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): The damage isn’t as good as a Sneak Attack, but rogues don’t have a lot of spells which require Concentration so if you can set this up before combat or on a turn when you can’t deliver a Sneak Attack it could provide some useful area control.
-   Dancing Lights ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): An amusing distraction, but you can usually accomplish the same thing using Mage Hand and a candle or torch.
-   Elementalism ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Bad.
-   Encode Thoughts[](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/): Too situational. It’s a neat idea for a thieves’ guild to use this, but you just can’t afford space for it.
-   Fire Bolt ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Spell attacks won’t help you.
-   Friends ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Despite being built around Intelligence, you might make room for Charisma-based skills. An easy way to quickly charm a creature can be very helpful, though watch our for the 1-minute duration and the consequences when it ends.
-   Frostbite ([XGtE](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Maybe useful once you get Magical Ambush, but it’s a stretch.
-   Green-Flame Blade ([SCAG / TCoE](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Allows you to attack two enemies at once, which rogues are generally bad at doing. But you can’t guarantee that enemies will be positioned for the secondary damage, so this is inherently situational.
-   Gust ([EEPC](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): The push effect is too weak to justify. This could be useful if it didn’t have Verbal components because you could use it to create distractions while sneaking, but you still need to break stealth to cast it.
-   Infestation ([XGtE](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Not useful enough to use instead of attacking.
-   Light ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Get Darkvision. You’re a Rogue.
-   Lightning Lure ([SCAG / TCoE](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Not useful enough to use instead of attacking
-   Mage Hand ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): You get it for free.
-   Mending ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Too situational.
-   Message ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Conceptually great for a Rogue, but it’s only situationally useful.
-   Mind Sliver ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): An amazing cantrip, but it won’t deliver Sneak Attack.
-   Minor Illusion ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Fantastically useful.
-   Mold Earth ([EEPC](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Bad.
-   Poison Spray ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Spell attacks won’t help you.
-   Prestidigitation ([PHB](/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Versatile and fantastic, a creative rogue can do a lot between this and Minor Illusion. For suggestions on how to use Prestidigitation to its fullest, see our [Practical Guide to Prestidigitation](/2024-dnd/characters/prestidigitation).
-   Ray of Frost ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Spell attacks won’t help you.
-   Shape Water ([EEPC](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): A fantastically useful utility.
-   Shocking Grasp ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Spell attacks won’t help you.
-   Sword Burst ([SCAG / TCoE](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Run into a crowd, Sword Burst, Cunning Action (Disengage).
-   Thunderclap ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Bad.
-   Toll the Dead ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Fine damage, but it won’t deliver Sneak Attack.
-   True Strike ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): The go-to attack option for ranged builds, True Strike allows you to build around Intelligence and attack both in melee and at range equally well. Booming Blade may have more tactical impact and potentially better damage, but it only works in melee.

Still, in those situations ideally you have a tanky ally like a fighter to draw attention, or you’re using other spells like Blur or Mirror Image to protect yourself.

-   Sleep ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): A powerful save-or-suck spell at any level. Once you have Magical Ambush, you can incapacitate one or more creatures turn 1 and potentially remove them from the fight entirely.
-   Spellfire Flare ([FRHoF](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Spell attacks will not help you.
-   Snare ([XGtE](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Not useful enough to prepare permanently.
-   Tasha’s Caustic Brew ([TCoE](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Not enough damage.
-   Thunderwave ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): If you need to get away from enemies, Cunning Action (Disengage).
-   Unseen Servant ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): A nice utility, but not consistently useful enough to prepare permanently.
-   Wardaway ([FRHoF](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): A weaker version of Tasha’s Mind Whip, Wardaway makes some huge trades to fit into a level 1 spell. The damage is obviously much worse, but that’s not why you’re here. The target still gets to move an act in the same turn, but halving their speed is still impactful. The biggest limitation is that Wardaway uses a Constitution save instead of a much more reliable Intelligence save.
    
    At low levels where enemies have very few saving throw proficiencies, Wardaway may be decently reliable. But enemies at low levels also rarely have Bonus Actions, so they’re likely to fail the save and barely notice the spell’s effect.
    
    If you are going to upcast this, cast Tasha’s Mind Whip instead.
    
-   Witch Bolt ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Spell attacks won’t help you.

-   Spray of Cards ([BOmT](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): The damage is poor, but temporarily blinding a creature on a Dexterity save is potentially powerful against Strength-based enemies, who typically have awful Dexterity saves.

Jump out of a barrel and shout “go take the day off” and watch your target load up their belongings and go home for the day.
    
    The 2024 version of the spell is insanely abusable because of how permissive it is. RAW you can tell a creature to suffocate themselves to death. I recommend using the 2014 version of the spell to avoid Suggestion derailing your game.
    
-   Tasha’s Mind Whip ([TCoE](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Not a lot of damage, but it’s on an Intelligence save, and it severely limits your target’s action options, making this a powerful offensive option at any level.
-   Vortex Warp ([SCoC](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Very good, but it doesn’t affect you, so this is best left to full spellcasters.
-   Warding Wind ([XGtE](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): A moving sphere of difficult terrain centered on you. This can make it difficult for enemies to get into melee with you, which can make hit-and-run tactics very simple. But you may be better served by using Cunning Action to move in and out of range of your enemies’ movement.
-   Web ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Fantastic area control at any level even if your Intelligence is relatively poor.

-   Ashardalon’s Stride ([FToD](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): You have Cunning Action if you need to move around without provoking Opportunity Attacks.

-   Conjure Constructs ([FRHoF](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): The damage and the Action economy are terrible, but you might use this between encounters to provide Temporary Hit Points to your whole party. -   Enemies Abound ([XgtE](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Astoundingly few enemies have good Intelligence saves, especially big scary melee monsters. Throw this on something tanky and horrifying that’s there to protect squishy enemies from you and your friends, and watch it freak out and kill its buddies for you. The duration is only a minute, and obviously this only works in an encounter with multiple enemies, but that doesn’t make the spell less awesome.
-   Erupting Earth ([EEPC / XGtE](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Not enough damage to compete with Fireball, and not enough difficult terrain to justify the spell slot.
-   Fast Friends ([AI](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): You won’t use this enough to justify one of your extremely limited preparation slots. Cast Suggestion instead.
-   Fear ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): A great way to disable groups of opponents, but fear immunity is common. Somewhere an Arcane Trickster has made a habit of using Magical Ambush to leap from cover, shout “Boo!”, and cause entire encounters to drop their weapons and flee.
-   Feign Death ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Too situational.
-   Fireball ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Rogues are very good at killing big, singular enemies, but they’re bad at handling crowds of weak enemies. Fireball neatly fills that gap. Even if you’re surrounded, you’re proficient in Dexterity saves and have Evasion. Drop a Fireball at you’re feet, and you’ll probably come out in better shape than your enemies will.
-   Flame Arrows ([XGtE](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Bad. A total of 12d6 damage is not enough for a level 3 spell slot, and that’s assuming that you manage to make 12 qualifying attacks in the 1-hour duration and hit with them. A Rogue is arguably the most likely to make this work, but it’s just not enough damage.
-   Fly ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): It’s hard to overstate how powerful flight is.
-   Gaseous Form ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): If anyone were to use this to great effect, it would be a Rogue. But it’s still only situationally useful, so it’s hard to justify preparing it permanently.
-   Glyph of Warding ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Depending on how your DM handles it, this is either a situationally useful defensive measure or a reality-bending way to break the game from the comfort of your own home. See our [Practical Guide to Glyph of Warding](https://rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/characters/spells/glyph-of-warding/).
-   Haste ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Used as intended, Haste is a decent short-term buff that will give you either some extra movement or an additional chance to hit if your one attack per turn misses. In most cases, you can get the same benefits from your Bonus Action by using Cunning Action for movement or Two-Weapon Fighting for an additional attack.
    
    But Haste also has an abuse case which you can use to reliably get a Sneak Attack outside of your turn. On your own turn, use the bonus Action from Haste to attack. If you miss, you’ve still taken the Attack action on your turn, so you still qualify for two-weapon fighting if you need another attack (and provided that you meet the other requirements). Then spend your normal Action to take the Ready action.
    
    Use a sufficiently broad condition like “anyone other than me moves or acts”, and choose to attack as your response to the condition. Then when someone else tries to do something you can attack using your Reaction, potentially dealing another Sneak Attack (remember that Sneak Attack is once per turn, not once per round).
    
-   Hypnotic Pattern ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Use Magical Ambush, save-or-suck the entire encounter, then quickly eliminate enemies one at a time. Barring resistance/immunity to Charmed, you’re basically unstoppable.
-   Intellect Fortress ([TCoE](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Technically situational, but an absolutely spectacular defense against enemies which rely on spells or common effects like charm and fear effects. Unlike species traits like the Gnome’s Cunning or the Satyr and Yuan-Ti’s Magic Resistance, this applies to _all_ Intelligence, Wisdom, and Charisma saving throws, providing broad and effective protection against many of the most dangerous save-or-suck effects in the game. You also get resistance to Psychic damage, which is nice if you’re fighting mind flayers, aboleths, or Bards who enjoy Vicious Mockery. With a 1-hour duration, the Concentration requirement can be problematic, but it also means that you can carry this through multiple encounters at low cost, so in situations where you need this it’s not going to eat all of your spell slots.
-   Laerel’s Silver Lance ([FRHoF](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): The damage type is fantastic, the Prone effect is great, very few area damage spells target Strength saves, and you can omit targets to avoid hitting friends. There’s definitely a niche here, but 3d10 (avg. 16.5) is very low compared to other level 3 spells. Lightning Bolt is 8d6 (avg. 28), and upcasting Laerel’s Silver Lance actually widens that gap. Preparing this and buying the 250 gp spell component feels like too much investment for the extremely narrow case where you can justify using this over other damage spells.
-   Leomund’s Tiny Hut ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Tiny Hut is a great place to rest, and you can cast it as a ritual. Leave it to a full caster if you can.
-   Lightning Bolt ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Line AOEs are really hard. Use Fireball.
-   Magic Circle ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Too situational to prepare permanently
-   Major Image ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Fantastically versatile, and creatures don’t make a saving throw. Instead, they need to know to touch the illusion or make an Intelligence (Investigation) check, or they need to physically interact with the illusion. Even then, you can buy yourself a great deal of time while the target tries to figure out your illusion.
-   Melf’s Minute Meteors ([EEPC / XGtE](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Conflicts with Cunning Action.
-   Nondetection ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Good, but not totally essential. Divination spells include things like See Invisibility, so if you or your party relies on invisibility of any kind this protects from several magical countermeasures to both stealth and invisibility. However, most enemies aren’t spellcasters and won’t have access to those divination options, so you can’t justify casting this every day. The spell also has an expensive material component specifically to deter you from casting this all the time. Still, with an 8-hour duration, if you need this spell it’s going to do exactly what you need it to do.
-   Phantom Steed ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): By the time you can cast this, your allies can likely produce much better ways to travel long distances.
-   Protection from Energy ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): You have Evasion and you can cast Absorb Elements. You won’t get enough out of this to justify preparing it permanently.
-   Remove Curse ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Too situational to prepare permanently.
-   Sending ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Too situational to prepare permanently.
-   Sleet Storm ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Not useful enough to make it more appealing than Fireball or Hypnotic Pattern.
-   Slow ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): A good debuff, but it won’t help you do Rogue things.
-   Speak with Dead ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Too situational to prepare permanently.
-   Spirit Shroud ([TCoE](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): With only one or two attacks per turn, this isn’t worth the spell slot.
-   Stinking Cloud ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): While this can rob targets of their Action if they fail the save, the area is small and easy to escape.
-   Summon Fey ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Decent summons, but no unique synergy. The Tricksy version’s ability to summon magical darkness is neat, but it doesn’t help you since you can’t see in magical darkness.
    
    For more help, see our [Practical Guide to Summoning Spells](https://rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/characters/summoning#summon-).
    
-   Summon Lesser Demons ([XGtE](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): You can’t control the demons, and they’re probably not strong enough to win an encounter on their own. The material component may also be problem since it has a 24 freshness timer.
    
    For more help, see our [Practical Guide to Summoning Spells](https://rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/characters/summoning#summon-lesser-demons).
    
-   Summon Shadowspan ([TCoE](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): The sneakiest summon option, but the ability to hide as a Bonus Action sometimes does not make this any better for a Rogue than for anyone else.
    
    For more help, see our [Practical Guide to Summoning Spells](https://rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/characters/summoning#summon-shadowspawn).
    
-   Summon Undead ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Several excellent combat options, and the ghostly option can fly around and do stuff for you. The Skeletal option is an effective ranged attacker, and it can easily out damage any of your other spell options if you can fit multiple encounters into the 1-hour duration.
    
    For more help, see our [Practical Guide to Summoning Spells](https://rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/characters/summoning#summon-undead).
    
-   Syluné’s Viper ([FRHoF](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Bonus Action casting time, 1-hour duration, no Concentration. The damage is horrid, and poison immunity is obviously a problem, but the ability to make a target Incapacitated on hit means that you can lock a single target out of a fight for as long as you can reliably hit them with attacks. The Incapacitation effect doesn’t allow a save, so even Legendary Resistance won’t protect enemies from this. You also get a climb speed so that you can climb out of reach and bite enemies from safety.
    
    The spell’s biggest weakness is the Temporary Hit Points. You only get 15, and the spell ends early once you’re out of THP. Plan to hide as a Bonus Action to make yourself hard to target and also to get Advantage on the Spell Attack.
    
-   Thunder Step ([XGtE](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Use Misty Step and reserve your Action for attacking.
-   Tongues ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): If you’re built to be decent in social situations, this could be useful, but most Arcane Tricksters dump Charisma and related skills.

-   Wall of Sand ([EEPC / XGtE](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Passable area control, but Web will be more effective if the terrain supports it.
-   Wall of Water ([EEPC / XGtE](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): This makes it hard for you to engage enemies. Wall of Sand is more useful.
-   Water Breathing ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Too situational to prepare permanently.

### Level 4 Arcane Trickster Rogue Spells

-   Arcane Eye ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Scouting safely is important, and it’s a major part of the Rogue’s role in the party. Still, you’ve been scouting for 18 levels without this and you’ve somehow survived this long.
-   Backlash ([FRHoF](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): You have Uncanny Dodge and Evasion, which should cover the vast majority of damage source which might justify using this. If you want additional protection, prepare Absorb Elements and/or Shield.
-   Banishment ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): A fantastic save-or-suck, especially with Magical Ambush. Start an encounter by banishing the biggest thing in the fight that doesn’t have Legendary Resistance, them clean up whatever’s left before your target can come back.
-   Blight ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Bad.
-   Charm Monster ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): If you’re trying to be friendly, use Enhance Ability. If you’re trying to compel and enemy to do something you want, use Suggestion.
-   Confusion ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Unpredictable and unreliable.
-   Conjure Minor Elementals ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Fantastic more many characters relying on attack rolls, but you’re likely only making two attacks per turn at most, so you won’t benefit as much as most characters who would use this.
-   Control Water ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Too situational.
-   Dimension Door ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): While Misty Step solves the vast majority of your teleportation needs (especially in combat), Dimension Door has considerably longer range and doesn’t require line of sight, thus allowing you to quickly infiltrate or escape an area which might otherwise be impenetrable.
-   Divination ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Too situational to prepare permanently.
-   Fabricate ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Too situational to prepare permanently.
-   Fire Shield ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): You should not be drawing enough attacks to justify this.
-   Greater Invisibility ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Probably the most important spell that anyone can cast on a rogue.
-   Hallucinatory Terrain ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Unless you specifically need the 24-hour duration and the massive area of effect, Major Image will suffice.
-   Ice Storm ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Terrible. Bad damage, and the difficult terrain doesn’t last long enough.
-   Leomund’s Secret Chest ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Too situational to prepare permanently.
-   Locate Creature ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Too situational to prepare permanently.
-   Mordenkainen’s Faithful Hound ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Decent for defending a specific point, but not useful enough to be your only level 4 spell each day.

-   Otiluke’s Resilient Sphere ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Single-target save-or-suck, and you can make it more reliable with Magical Ambush. A good way to take a target out of a fight until you’re ready to handle them.
-   Phantasmal Killer ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): If you’re going to hit a single target with a spell, you want it to eliminate or incapacitate them in one shot, and Phantasmal Killer simply doesn’t do that.
-   Polymorph ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Normally a fantastic combat option, but your own attacks will greatly exceed anything that you can turn into. You might turn into something just for the massive pool of Temporary Hit Points, but that’s likely not enough to justify your only level 4 spell slot.
-   Raulothim’s Psychic Lance ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Too situational to prepare permanently.
-   [Sickening Radiance](https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/14597-sickening-radiance) ([XGtE](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): This spell is very easy to overlook. The effects are complicated, and the 4d10 damage looks underwhelming, but don’t let that deter you. This is a great spell to cast into a room then shut the door, but, even if that’s not an option, it’s a fantastic way to handle crowds. With a 30-foot radius you can hit a huge number of targets, and with a 10-minute duration you can easily kill anything stuck in the area for an extended period.
    
    The 4d10 damage is fine, and negating invisibility is great, but the real appeal is the levels of Exhaustion. The penalties on d20 tests and to speed make it progressively easier to keep enemies in the area, and pushing enemies back into the area will get progressively easier the more times you do it. Enemies will either die from gradual damage or from hitting 6 levels of Exhaustion as the stacking penalties to their d20 tests make the save functionally impossible to pass.
    
-   Spellfire Storm ([FRHoF](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): The damage isn’t great and Constitution saves aren’t reliable, but the AOE is decently large and this can prevent enemies from casting spells. The Con save when casting a spell is identical to Counterspell, so you’re effectively countering every spell that enemies try to cast while in the area. I would only use when facing enemy spellcasters (the damage isn’t good enough otherwise), but with enemy spellcasters on the field this can easily define an encounter.
    
    We can also compare Spellfire Storm to Moonbeam, which also creates a column of Radiant damage. Spellfire Storm deals the same damage as Moonbeam cast at the same spell level and uses the same save, but instead of bother Shapeshifters, Spellfire Storm shuts down enemy spellcasters. Spellfire Storm can’t be moved, but the considerably larger radius means that you don’t need to move it in many encounters. In close quarters, enemies may be unable to move out of the area. Of course, that also means that you can’t use forced movement to repeatedly trigger the spell’s damage like you could with area effects which don’t cover the entire battlefield.
    
    Casting as a Circle Spell: 25,000 gp to do this is absolutely insane. This is something that should happen as a plot point, not as a player tactic. Maybe you drop this on an extraplanar gate so that your party can fight waves of demons to shut down the gate or something similarly epic and plot-defining.
    
-   Spirit of Death ([BoMT](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Summon Undead is arguably better, but this is still a good summon.

-   Stone Shape ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): This is one of the most useful spells in the game. This is a spell limited only by your imagination. A 5-foot cube is plenty of space to wreak all kinds of havoc if you’re clever.

-   Summon Aberration ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Three diverse and effective combat options. Even barring that, summons are pretty great in combat.

-   Summon Construct ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Not as effective offensively as Summon Aberration, but all three summoned constructs are unusually durable for creatures summoned by similar spells.
    
    For more help, see our [Practical Guide to Summoning Spells](https://rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/characters/summoning#summon-construct).
    
-   Summon Elemental ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): Summon Aberration is better offensively, and Summon Construct provides a more durable summon. -   Watery Sphere ([EEPC / XGtE](//rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/abbreviations/)): The primary appeal here is the ability to restrain the target, then drag them around for the spell’s duration, but that consumes your Action, which means that you’re not Sneak Attacking.
