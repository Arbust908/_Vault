## Introduction

The Eldritch Knight Fighter’s spellcasting is limited, but still very powerful. Alongside the Eldritch Knight’s class and subclass features, limited access to the Wizard’s spell list gives the Fighter a lot of options.

Eldritch Knights can only replace a single prepared spell each time that you gain a Fighter level, and you can prepare a maximum of 13 spells at level 20. This means that choosing effective spells is crucial, especially late in your career when you’ll have little time to correct a poor choice. War Magic and Improved War Magic also significantly impact your decisions. Remember: you are a Fighter first, not a Wizard.

This article primarily caters to Eldritch Knight Fighters, but other martial characters with the Magic Initiate feat may also find it helpful.

## Shillelagh and Why It’s Important

While it’s not on the Wizard’s spell list, Shillelagh is available as an Intelligence-based spell via the Magic Initiate feat. Since it’s available from level 1, you can build around Shillelagh immediately. This does limit you to the Slow and Topple Weapon Masteries, but, once you gain Tactical Master at level 9, that becomes less of an issue since you can use Push, Sap, and Slow regardless of weapon.

This has a huge impact on the Eldritch Knight’s spell selection. At the cost of limiting yourself to exactly two weapon choices, you can build around Intelligence almost entirely. This makes your spell attacks and save DCs comparable to those of a Wizard or any other full spellcaster, which is why it features heavily in our [example build](https://rpgbot.net/2024-dnd/classes/fighter/fighter-subclasses/eldritch-knight/#example).

At the same time, you have enough spell options that you could dump Intelligence and still do just fine. If you want to use other weapons, you can still be very effective. But, between Shillelagh’s scaling damage and simplifying your ability score needs, Shillelagh is objectively easier than relying on Strength or Dexterity.

## Eldritch Knight Fighter Spells

### Eldritch Knight Fighter Cantrips

The Eldritch Knight’s level 7 feature, War Magic, makes your cantrips extremely impactful. Unfortunately, you’ll never get more than 3 unless you get them from somewhere else (species, Magic Initiate, multiclass). You likely don’t have room for utility cantrips.

-   Acid Splash ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Great for handling groups of enemies, especially if you can force them into a small area with a Shove weapon. Unfortunately, you need to be built around Intelligence to use this reliably, which means Shillelagh, and Shillelagh doesn’t support any weapons with the Shove Weapon Mastery.
-   Blade Ward ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Excellent for any melee build. -1d4 to attacks against you is a significant AC boost. It does require Concentration, so you can’t combine it with many other useful buffs, but, once you have War Magic this is an easy choice in almost any encounter.
-   Booming Blade ([SCAG / TCoE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): The go-to attack cantrip for melee builds, Booming Blade is a defining tactical option for the Eldritch Knight. Combining this with the Push mastery allows you to knock enemies out of reach and leave them to take the secondary damage if they want to get back into melee with you.
-   Chill Touch ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Preventing healing is rarely impactful. See True Strike, below.
-   Control Flames[](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/): Bad. You don’t have space for utility, and this would still be bad if you did.
-   Create Bonfire[](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/): Decent low-budget area control. If you can grapple enemies to hold them in place or use a Push weapon to knock them into the fire, this can be easy damage.
-   Dancing Lights ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): You don’t have space for utility.
-   Elementalism ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): You don’t have space for utility.
-   Encode Thoughts[](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/): You don’t have space for utility.
-   Fire Bolt ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Intelligence-focused builds should use True Strike with a Longbow or a thrown weapon, and other builds shouldn’t rely on spell attacks. Fire Bolt’s damage will eventually outpace True Strike, but it won’t benefit from feats that you’re using to improve your attacks with weapons.
-   Friends ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): You don’t have space for utility, though this is a tempting way to imrpove the Fighter’s meager social capabilities.
-   Frostbite ([XGtE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Use a Sap weapon.
-   Green-Flame Blade ([SCAG / TCoE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Booming Blade is more useful for the Eldritch Knight’s role as a front-line tank, and you only get a total of 3 cantrips over your entire career, so it’s hard to justify taking both. The ability to damage two enemies with one attack is really enticing, but, if you need that functionality, you can either attack twice or cast Sword Burst.
    
    Note that Tasha’s Cauldron of Everything published an updated version of Green-Flame Blade.
    
-   Gust ([EEPC](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Use a Push weapon.
-   Infestation ([XGtE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Use a Push weapon.
-   Light ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): You don’t have space for utility.
-   Lightning Lure ([SCAG / TCoE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Despite the 15-foot range, this can be a great option for melee Eldritch Knights. Against enemies with poor Strength (like many enemy spellcasters), you can use this to drag them into melee with you and force them to teleport or Disengage in order to get away from you unharmed. If you’re flying, you may even be able to pull enemies into the air to cause a small amount of falling damage. Since it only has Verbal components, you can easily use this without juggling your weapon. The save is Strength, so you’ll only be successful with this against physically weak foes.
-   Mage Hand ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): You don’t have space for utility.
-   Mending ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): You don’t have space for utility.
-   Message ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): You don’t have space for utility.
-   Mind Sliver ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Even if you’re not building around Intelligence, Mind Sliver is fantastic. Intelligence saves are consistently the lowest save on average, so the damage and the debuff are reliable even if your save DC is mediocre. You could use this before hitting an enemy with an Unarmed Strike to grapple/shove them, or before you hit them with a Topple weapon, or you might just use it to support your allies.
-   Minor Illusion ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): You don’t have space for utility.
-   Mold Earth ([EEPC](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): You don’t have space for utility.
-   Poison Spray ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Excellent damage, but only appealing for the big damage dice, which aren’t enough. See True Strike, below.
-   Prestidigitation ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): You don’t have space for utility.
-   Ray of Frost ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): 2024 DnD’s character options offer numerous ways to forcibly move enemies and to reduce their speed. Combining this with the Slasher feat can allow you to reduce a creature’s speed by 20 feet. If you can do this and then move out of reach, many enemies will be totally unable to get back into melee with you, effectively preventing them from fighting. I wouldn’t take this on its own, but if your party is capitalizing on forced movement and speel debuffs, it’s a good choice.
-   Shape Water ([EEPC](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): You don’t have space for utility.
-   Shocking Grasp ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Preventing Opportunity Attacks could mean getting out of melee unimpeded, but you’re almost certainly built to endure an Opportunity Attack in a rare circumstance where you’re in melee against your will.
-   Sword Burst ([SCAG / TCoE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Eldritch Knights are typically in melee, and Sword Burst is a great way to handle crowds of multiple enemies.
-   Thunderclap ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Cast Sword Burst. It’s a better damage type on a more reliable save.
-   Toll the Dead ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Good damage, decent range, and a good damage type, but True Strike will still be a better choice in the vast majority of cases.
-   True Strike ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): True Strike is uniquely powerful in a way that defines your other cantrip options. Because it works both in melee and at range, you can use it as a go-to attack cantrip regardless of your choice of weaponry. For an Intelligence-based build, it’s perfect. It doesn’t provide the tactical utility that Booming Blade does, but it remains equally effective both at range and in melee.
    
    If you’re building around Strength or Dexterity, True Strike is largely useless. You _must_ use your spellcasting ability with True Strike, which means significantly worse accuracy and damage. Use Booming Blade instead.
    

### Level 1 Eldritch Knight Fighter Spells

-   Absorb Elements ([XGtE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): You get spellcasting later and slower than anyone else, so you have very few options for protecting yourself from non-weapon damage. This will handle that issue, but it will eat your spell slots quickly, so be sure to eliminate the source of the elemental damage as quickly as possible so that you don’t run through your spell slots too quickly.
    
    The biggest challenge for Absorb Elements is that it has Somatic components, which require a free hand. By default, this means that two-weapon fighting builds and sword-and-board builds will be unable to use this unless you’re diligent about drawing and stowing your weapon, which both makes opportunity attacks hard. Unfortunately, the only ways around this are [War Caster](https://rpgbot.net/dnd5/characters/feats/war-caster/) or Subtle Spell.
    
-   Alarm ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Not consistently useful enough to justify. If you need this, take Ritual Caster.
-   Burning Hands ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): The damage is good for a 1st-level spell if you can hit multiple foes in the AOE.
-   Catapult ([EEPC](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): The damage isn’t worth the spell slot by the time you get it.
-   Charm Person ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Helpful in social situations, but Advantage on Charisma checks is only minimally helpful when you have 8 Charisma and no social skills.
-   Chromatic Orb ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Obsolete by the time you get it.
-   Color Spray ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Bad.
-   Comprehend Languages ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Only situationally useful. Consider taking Ritual Caster if you want this.
-   Detect Magic ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Essential, but hopefully someone else in the party can cast it. Consider Ritual Caster if you really need it.
-   Disguise Self ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Only situationally useful. You won’t get enough use out of this to justify having it permanently prepared.
-   Distort Value ([AI](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Too situational.
-   Earth Tremor ([EEPC / XGtE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Not nearly enough damage, and being prone isn’t enough of a problem in 5e. The difficult terrain effect is the real draw. It only works on “loose earth or stone”, which may be a problematic limitation. Unfortunately, it also affects you, so be careful not to trap yourself among enemies.
-   Expeditious Retreat ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Nice mobility for melee builds, but Jump is a better choice so that you can also move vertically.
-   False Life ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): A little bit of extra durability, but probably not worth the spell slot.
-   Feather Fall ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Essential in any party, but wait to take it until you have enough combat spells to get through the day.
-   Find Familiar ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Get an owl and have it use Flyby and Help to give you Advantage before you try to hit an enemy with Booming Blade or another spell attack.
-   Fog Cloud ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Decent area control. If you have Fighting Style (Blind Fighting), this can be a go-to tactic.
-   Frost Fingers ([RotF](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Bad damage on a bad save.
-   Grease ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Decent area control, but not a big AOE. Consider using a Push weapon to repeatedly force enemies into the are to make them reattempt the saving throw.
-   Ice Knife ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Poor damage with too many points of failure.
-   Identify ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Only situationally useful. Consider Ritual Caster if you absolutely need this.
-   Illusory Script ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Too situational.
-   Jim’s Magic Missile[](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/): Usually no better than Magic Missile, but very powerful oncey you have both get Spirit Shroud and Improved War Magic.
-   Jump ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): A great boost to your mobility with a Bonus Action casting time. You can’t afford to use this in every encounter, but it’s great if you need to get past obstacles or hazards.
-   Longstrider ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): A great buff for melee builds.
-   Mage Armor ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): If you’re Dexterity-based, this is the only way to match the base AC provided by plate armor. The 8-hour duration is especially nice since you get so few spell slots to throw around.
-   Magic Missile ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Obsolete by the time you get it.
-   Protection from Evil and Good ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): A fantastic defensive buff against a wide variety of enemies. Once you get Improved War Magic at level 18, it’s easy to cast this in combat.
-   Ray of Sickness ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Poison resistance/immunity is common. For most of your career you can’t justify spending an Action to cast this, and by the time you get Improved War Magic your other options are much more impactful.
-   Shield ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): A fantastic defense, but you have very few spell slots, so you need to be very cautious about using it too frequently. If you’re in melee and use this a lot, consider the Defensive Duelist feat.
    
    The biggest challenge for Shield is that is has Somatic components, which require a free hand. By default, this means that two-weapon fighting builds and sword-and-board builds will be unable to use this unless you’re diligent about drawing and stowing your weapon, which both makes opportunity attacks hard and only allows Absorb Elements every other round. Unfortunately, the only way around this is [War Caster](https://rpgbot.net/dnd5/characters/feats/war-caster/).
    
-   Silent Image ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): A fantastic utility, but you probably can’t afford space for it in your limited number of prepared spells.
-   Silvery Barbs ([SCoC](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Extremely powerful, but also very complicated. See my [blog post on Silvery Barbs](https://rpgbot.net/dnd-5es-silvery-barbs/) for details on the numerous abuse cases which the spell allows. Unfortunately, it’s outside of the Eldritch Knight’s school limitations.
-   Sleep ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): A good save-or-suck at any level. Incapacitated effectively robs the target of their turn, and it also breaks Concentration.
-   Snare ([XGtE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Good for setting a trap, or if you’re resting somewhere dangerous with a convenient choke point like a narrow hallway. Targets are restrained for at least one round, which is hopefully enough for you to fight your way out of your bedroll and grab a weapon.
-   Spellfire Flare ([FRHoF](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Potentially useful at high levels when combined with Conjure Minor Elementals and Improved War Magic.
-   Tasha’s Caustic Brew ([TCoE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): The damage is too low by the time you can cast this.
-   Thunderwave ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Use a Push weapon.
-   Unseen Servant ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Too situational. Consider Ritual Caster if you really want this.
-   Wardaway ([FRHoF](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): A weaker version of Tasha’s Mind Whip, Wardaway makes some huge trades to fit into a level 1 spell. The damage is obviously much worse, but that’s not why you’re here. The target still gets to move an act in the same turn, but halving their speed is still impactful. The biggest limitation is that Wardaway uses a Constitution save instead of a much more reliable Intelligence save.
    
    At low levels where enemies have very few saving throw proficiencies, Wardaway may be decently reliable. But enemies at low levels also rarely have Bonus Actions, so they’re likely to fail the save and barely notice the spell’s effect.
    
    If you are going to upcast this, cast Tasha’s Mind Whip instead.
    
-   Witch Bolt ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): The secondary damage on a Bonus Action is tempting in extended fights, but casting this as an Action will rarely be worth how much damage you give up by casting this instead of attacking. When you get Improved War Magic at level 18, the Bonus Action damage is irrelevant.

### Level 2 Eldritch Knight Fighter Spells

-   Aganazzar’s scorcher ([XGtE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): I’ve never been a fan of line AOEs. It’s too difficult to line up more than two creatures at a time, so you’ll probably never hit more than two creatures unless you have a long hallway handy.
-   Air Bubble[](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/): Too situational.
-   Alter Self ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Only situationally useful.
-   Arcane Lock ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Too situational.
-   Arcane Vigor ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): This is what Second Wind is for.
-   Augury ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): One of my favorite divinations, but not useful enough for an Eldritch Knight to prepare it permanently.
-   Blindness/Deafness ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): A great debuff, but Con saves are hard, especially if you’re not built around Intelligence.
-   Blur ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): A great defensive buff.
-   Borrowed Knowledge[](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/): Combined with Tactical Knowledge, this is a great way to cover skills that your party can’t already handle.
-   Cloud of Daggers ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Cast Create Bonfire.
-   Continual Flame ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Too situational.
-   Crown of Madness ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Too situational.
-   Darkness ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Without the ability to see in magical darkness, Fog Cloud provides the same benefit.
-   Darkvision ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Great if you don’t already have Darkvision, but very expensive with your limited resources. Get Goggles of Night if you can.

-   Dragon’s Breath[](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/): Your attacks will do more damage than spending your Action to use the breath weapon. This may be appealing if you can cast it on a familiar.

-   Earthbind[](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/): Throw a trident.
-   Elminster’s Elusion ([FRHoF](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Situational by design since you only need this against spells and magic effects, which not every enemy can produce. This can help a lot with saves which are normally very dangerous for martial characters, though you’ll probably still need Indomitable for low mental saves.
-   Enhance Ability ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): A fantastic utility outside of combat.
-   Enlarge/Reduce ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): The increased reach and the damage boost can be useful.
-   Flaming Sphere ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): You could use this as a sort of area control combined with Push and/or Slow weapons in order to hold enemies in place adjacent to the sphere to force additional damage. But by the time you can cast this, Create Bonfire will do as much damage and can be cast as part of your Attack thanks to War Magic.
-   Gentle Repose ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Too situational.
-   Gift of Gab ([AI](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): YOu are not a Face.
-   Gust of Wind ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Too situational.
-   Hold Person ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Only works against humanoids, who will be rare enemies by the time that you can cast this.
-   Invisibility ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Excellent. A Dexterity-based build may be a very effective scout if you can also cast Invisibility.
-   Jim’s Glowing Coin[](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/): If you can cast this before enemies notice you, it can put your party at a huge advantage. But you can’t always expect that to be an option.
-   Kinetic Jaunt ([SCoC](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Great mobility, but not significantly more useful than Jump.
-   Knock ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Very useful, but your party needs to be able to handle locks long before you can cast this.
-   Levitate ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Constitution saves are hard, but lifting an enemy off the ground makes many enemies much less dangerous, often totally removing them from a fight.
-   Locate Object ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Too situational to prepare permanently.
-   Magic Mouth ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Too situational to prepare permanently.
-   Magic Weapon ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): A fantastic offensive buff. It’s great at this level, and you can upcast it to increase the bonus to +2.
-   Maximilian’s Earthen Grasp ([EEPC](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): A great way to restrain small nimble enemies and weak spellcasters, both of which are likely to look for ways to run away from you.
-   Melf’s Acid Arrow ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Poor damage.
-   Mind Spike ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Too situational.
-   Mirror Image ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): An excellent defensive buff, especially once you have Improved War Magic.
-   Misty Step ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Absolutely fantastic. Eventually you’ll want to trade it out once you get Arcane Charge, but that’s not until 15th level.
-   Nathair’s Mischief ([FToD](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Unpredictable and weak.
-   Nystul’s Magic Aura ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Only situationally useful. Masking yourself as a construct makes you immune to many dangerous spells, but enemies rarely cast those spells.
-   Phantasmal Force ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): An excellent sav-eor-suck at any level, especially since it targets Intelligence saves.
-   Pyrotechnics ([EEPC](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): The effects are weak and the required setup is annoying.
-   Ray of Enfeeblement ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Con saves are the least reliable save, and the creatures that suffer from this spell will also have high Constitution saves.
-   Rope Trick ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Useful, but I really hope that someone else in your party can cast this.
-   Scorching Ray ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Rarely a good idea, but, once you have Improved War Magic, you can combine this with Conjure Minor Elementals to deal a ton of damage very quickly.
-   See Invisibility ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Probably your only counter to invisible enemies unless you can get a magic item like a Lantern of Revealing. Maybe skip this if your party has other options like Faerie Fire.
-   Shadow Blade ([XGtE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Compared to a rapier, the Shadow Blade adds 1d8 damage per attack, it changes to psychic damage (which is resisted by very few creatures), and it adds the Thrown property for some reason. Since it gives you Advantage to attack creatures in dim light or darkness it will offset Disadvantage for attacking creatures when it’s too dark for you to see. If you’re not built around Intelligence, this is a great boost to your damage output, and gives Strength-based and Dexterity-based builds an easy ranged option. Unfortunately, it doesn’t have Weapon Mastery because it was published under the 2014 rules.
-   Shatter ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Decent damage with a good AOE and Thunder damage. You’ll rarely use it in combat unless there is an extremely dense group of enemies.
-   Skywrite ([EEPC](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Too situational
-   Snilloc’s snowball swarm ([XGtE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Shatter but with less damage and worse damage type.
-   Spider Climb ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): The next best thing to flight. Very appealing for fighting at range.
-   Spray of Cards ([BOmT](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): The damage is poor, but temporarily blinding a creature on a Dexterity save is potentially powerful against Strength-based enemies, who typically have awful Dexterity saves.
-   Suggestion ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Fantastically versatile and extremely powerful.
    
    The 2024 version of the spell is insanely abusable because of how permissive it is. RAW you can tell a creature to suffocate themselves to death. I recommend using the 2014 version of the spell to avoid Suggestion derailing your game.
    
-   Tasha’s Mind Whip ([TCoE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Not a lot of damage, but it’s on an Intelligence save, and it severely limits your target’s action options, making this a powerful offensive option at any level.
-   Vortex Warp ([SCoC](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Very good, but you probably can’t afford to cast this often. It may be useful once you have Improved War Magic so that you can reposition and ally at reduced action cost.
-   Warding Wind ([XGtE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): A moving sphere of difficult terrain centered on you. This makes it easy for you to keep enemies close (especially if you use the Slow Weapon Mastery to further reduce their speed), and the 10-minute duration can get you through multiple fights.
-   Web ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Fantastic area control at any level even if your Intelligence is relatively poor.

### Level 3 Eldritch Knight Fighter Spells

-   Animate Dead ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): You don’t have enough spell slots to keep your undead under control.
-   Antagonize ([BoMT](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Attacking stuff is your job. This will rarely do more damage than your own attacks.
-   Ashardalon’s Stride ([FToD](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Bonus action casting time, and the damage doesn’t allow a save. This is a tempting spell for gish builds, but, by the time you can cast it, the damage is negligible. This is great for builds with better spellcasting than the Eldritch Knight, but for the Eldritch Knight you should look elsewhere.
-   Bestow Curse ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): A powerful debuff, but against msot creatures you’ll get better results from just killing them.
-   Blink ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): An interesting defensive option which doesn’t require Concentration, but, as a Fighter, you’re durable enough to stand up to enemy attacks. Removing yourself from the board half of the time means that you’re not there to take attacks in place of your weaker allies.
-   Catnap ([XGtE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Only situationally useful, and you should not be the one to cast it.
-   Clairvoyance ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): A great scouting option, but very expensive with the Eldritch Knight’s limited spellcasting.
-   Counterspell ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Helpful, but only situationally useful, and you almost certainly don’t have the room to prepare this on a daily basis without major sacrifices.
-   Dispel Magic ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): If your party doesn’t have someone else who can cast Dispel Magic by the time it’s an option for you, you’re probably all dead.
-   Enemies Abound ([XgtE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Astoundingly few enemies have good Intelligence saves, especially big scary melee monsters. Throw this on something tanky and horrifying that’s there to protect squishy enemies from you and your friends, and watch it freak out and kill its buddies for you. The duration is only a minute, and obviously this only works in an encounter with multiple enemies, but that doesn’t make the spell less awesome.
-   Erupting Earth ([EEPC / XGtE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Not enough damage to compete with Fireball, and not enough difficult terrain to justify the spell slot. If you need to slow down enemies, go stand in their way.
-   Fast Friends ([AI](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): You won’t use this enough to justify one of your extremely limited preparation slots. Cast Suggestion instead.
-   Fear ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): A great way to disable groups of opponents, but fear immunity is common.
-   Feign Death ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Too situational.
-   Fireball ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): At 8d6 damage, Fireball hitting multiple enemies will likely exceed the amount of damage you can do to a single creature until you get four attacks at 20th level.
-   Flame Arrows ([XGtE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Tempting for a fighter to boost your damage output, but it’s not good enough to consume one of your few spells known that go beyond your school limitations.
-   Fly ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Flight is a defining tactical advantage over creatures who can’t fly, especially if those creatures don’t have effective ranged options. Even though this is beyond the Eldritch Knight’s school limitations it’s a must-have option. If you don’t have easy access to flight by other means (an allied spellcaster, an item, a flying mount, etc.), you need this.
-   Gaseous Form ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Too situational to prepare permanently.
-   Glyph of Warding ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Depending on how your DM handles it, this is either a situationally useful defensive measure or a reality-bending way to break the game from the comfort of your own home. See our [Practical Guide to Glyph of Warding](https://rpgbot.net/dnd5/characters/spells/glyph-of-warding/).
-   Haste ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): An excellent buff at any level. The option to take an additional Attack action has a weird interaction with War Magic. Haste limits you to one attack, and War Magic lets you replace one attack with a cantrip when you take the Attack action on your turn. It appears that War Magic can replace the additional attack with a Cantrip.
-   Hypnotic Pattern ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Take a group of creatures out of a fight for a full minute on only one save. Targets don’t get another save, and the effect doesn’t end until the spell does or someone breaks targets out of the effect. This means that you can focus on enemies which pass the initial save, then gradually eliminate the remaining targets one at a time. This doesn’t scale with spell level, but it really doesn’t need to. A 30-foot cube is enough to hit several creatures, and so long as your Spell Save DC is decent you’ll do fine. Even if enemies spend an Action to break their allies out of the spell, you’ve spent one Action to incapacitate them and they’re spending more than that just to fix it without actually harming you or your allies.
-   Intellect Fortress ([TCoE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Technically situational, but an absolutely spectacular defense against enemies which rely on spells or common effects like charm and fear effects. Unlike species traits like the Gnome’s Cunning or the Satyr and Yuan-Ti’s Magic Resistance, this applies to _all_ Intelligence, Wisdom, and Charisma saving throws, providing broad and effective protection against many of the most dangerous save-or-suck effects in the game. You also get resistance to Psychic damage, which is nice if you’re fighting mind flayers, aboleths, or Bards who enjoy Vicious Mockery. With a 1-hour duration, the Concentration requirement can be problematic, but it also means that you can carry this through multiple encounters at low cost, so in situations where you need this it’s not going to eat all of your spell slots.
-   Laerel’s Silver Lance ([FRHoF](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): The damage type is fantastic, the Prone effect is great, very few area damage spells target Strength saves, and you can omit targets to avoid hitting friends. There’s definitely a niche here, but 3d10 (avg. 16.5) is very low compared to other level 3 spells. Lightning Bolt is 8d6 (avg. 28), and upcasting Laerel’s Silver Lance actually widens that gap. Preparing this and buying the 250 gp spell component feels like too much investment for the extremely narrow case where you can justify using this over other damage spells.
-   Leomund’s Tiny Hut ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Perhaps an odd choice for the Eldritch Knight, but Tiny Hut is a great place to rest, and you can cast it as a ritual. If you have time to set it up it’s a great defensive position.
-   Lightning Bolt ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): As much damage as fireball, but it’s much harder to hit multiple creatures with a line.
-   Magic Circle ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Too situational to prepare permanently
-   Major Image ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Powerful and versatile, but an Eldritch Knight likely won’t use it often enough to prepare permanently.
-   Melf’s Minute Meteors ([EEPC / XGtE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): I love that you can use this to turn your bonus action into damage output, but with your poor save DC this deals too little damage to waste a 3rd-level spell slot.
-   Nondetection ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Only situationally useful, and you typically cast this as a precaution at the beginning of an adventuring day. You may not be able to afford to spend a spell slot on this every day.
-   Phantom Steed ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): A wonderful utility, but you probably don’t want to prepare it permanently. Hopefully someone else in the party can cast this.
-   Protection from Energy ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): An excellent defensive option, but you may be doing alright with Absorb Elements.
-   Remove Curse ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Too situational to prepare permanently.
-   Sending ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Too situational to prepare permanently.
-   Sleet Storm ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Not useful enough to make it more appealing than Fireball or Hypnotic Pattern.
-   Slow ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): A good debuff, but not good enough to compare with other level 3 options when you get so few spells.
-   Speak with Dead ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Too situational to prepare permanently.
-   Spirit Shroud ([TCoE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): A 1-minute combat buff which adds 1d8 damage and a 10 ft. speed reduction to each of your numerous attacks. Combined with Action Surge, you can deal a huge amount of damage very quickly. This can be very powerful on a Defender build, and it’s easy to combine with the Slow Weapon Mastery to prevent enemies from getting away from you.
-   Stinking Cloud ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): While this can rob targets of their Action if they fail the save, the area is small and easy to escape.
-   Summon Fey ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Decent summons at this spell level, but no specific synergy.
    
    For more help, see our [Practical Guide to Summoning Spells](https://rpgbot.net/dnd5/characters/summoning#summon-).
    
-   Summon Lesser Demons ([XGtE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): You can’t control the demons, and they’re probably not strong enough to win an encounter on their own. The material component may also be problem since it has a 24 freshness timer.
    
    For more help, see our [Practical Guide to Summoning Spells](https://rpgbot.net/dnd5/characters/summoning#summon-lesser-demons).
    
-   Summon Shadowspan ([TCoE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): The Despair version’s speed debuff is great for keeping enemies in melee with you.
    
    For more help, see our [Practical Guide to Summoning Spells](https://rpgbot.net/dnd5/characters/summoning#summon-shadowspawn).
    
-   Summon Undead ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Several excellent combat options, and the ghostly option can fly around and do stuff for you. The Skeletal option is an effective ranged attacker, and it can easily outdamage any of your other spell options if you can fit multiple encounters into the 1-hour duration.
    
    For more help, see our [Practical Guide to Summoning Spells](https://rpgbot.net/dnd5/characters/summoning#summon-undead).
    
-   Syluné’s Viper ([FRHoF](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Bonus Action casting time, 1-hour duration, no Concentration. The damage is horrid, and poison immunity is obviously a problem, but the ability to make a target Incapacitated on hit means that you can lock a single target out of a fight for as long as you can reliably hit them with attacks. The Incapacitation effect doesn’t allow a save, so even Legendary Resistance won’t protect enemies from this. You also get a climb speed so that you can climb out of reach and bite enemies from safety.
    
    The spell’s biggest weakness is the Temporary Hit Points. You only get 15, and the spell ends early once you’re out of THP. You likely have good AC, but by this level any save-for-half damage effect will be enoug to cut right through the tiny pool of THP. You’ll only be able to use this safely against single targets that can’t deal damage with Lair Actions or Legendary Actions.
    
-   Thunder Step ([XGtE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): You can get most of the same teleportation function from Arcane Charge.
-   Tongues ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Too situational to prepare permanently.
-   Vampiric Touch ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): The healing is really cool, but spending your entire Action to use it is hard to justify.
-   Wall of Sand ([EEPC / XGtE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Passable area control, but Web will be more effective if the terrain supports it.
-   Wall of Water ([EEPC / XGtE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): This makes it hard for you to to engage enemies. Wall of Sand is more useful.
-   Water Breathing ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Too situational to prepare permanently.

### Level 4 Eldritch Knight Fighter Spells

-   Arcane Eye ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): An absolutely fantastic scouting option, but not worth your only level 4 spell slot.
-   Backlash ([FRHoF](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): You get exactly one level 4 spell slot, you have d10 hit dice, and you have Second Wind. You shouldn’t need this enough to justify preparing it.
-   Banishment ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Fantastic save-or-suck.
-   Blight ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Bad.
-   Charm Monster ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Helpful in social situations, but not consistently useful enough for you to prepare permanently.
-   Confusion ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Unpredictable and unreliable.
-   Conjure Minor Elementals ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Excellent, but you may have better results with Spirit Shroud. Spirit Shroud fills a similar nihe, and it’s cast as a Bonus Action. Conjure Minor Elementals is cast as an Action, but has a 10-minute duration compared to Spirit Shroud’s 1-minute duration, plus Conjure Minor Elementals has a 15-foot radius compared to Spirit Shroud’s 10-foot radius, and does more damage. Pick whichever works for you.
-   Control Water ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Too situational.
-   Dimension Door ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Use Arcane Charge.
-   Divination ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Too situational to prepare permanently.
-   Fabricate ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Too situational to prepare permanently.
-   Fire Shield ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): One of the Fighter’s primary roles is Defender. Your job is to stand in the front and get attacked by scary monsters. Throw up Fire Shield and enjoy a bunch of extra damage output for being in the right place and doing your job. Just be sure that enemies still have a good reason to attack you instead of your allies.
-   Greater Invisibility ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Invisibility is an absolutely spectacular buff, but part of the Fighter’s primary role in the party is to draw attention away from less durable allies. If you’re invisible, you can’t do that.
-   Hallucinatory Terrain ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Too situational to prepare permanently.
-   Ice Storm ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Terrible. Bad damage, and the difficult terrain doesn’t last long enough.
-   Leomund’s Secret Chest ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Too situational to prepare permanently.
-   Locate Creature ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Too situational to prepare permanently.
-   Mordenkainen’s Faithful Hound ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Decent for defending a specific point, but not useful enough to be your only level 4 spell each day.
-   Mordenkainen’s Private Sanctum ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): You get one level 4 spell slot every day. Do you really want to spend it on a place to sleep?
-   Otiluke’s Resilient Sphere ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Single-target save-or-suck. A good way to take a target out of a fight until you’re ready to handle them.
-   Phantasmal Killer ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): A decent single-target debuff against martial enemies, but fear immunity is common, and the damage won’t be as impressive by this level.
-   Polymorph ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): With an hour-long duration, this is a great use of your single 4th-level spell slot. See our [Practical Guide to Polymorph](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/characters/polymorph) for more information.
-   Raulothim’s Psychic Lance ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Too situational to prepare permanently.
-   [Sickening Radiance](https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/14597-sickening-radiance) ([XGtE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): This spell is very easy to overlook. The effects are complicated, and the 4d10 damage looks underwhelming, but don’t let that deter you. This is a great spell to cast into a room then shut the door, but, even if that’s not an option, it’s a fantastic way to handle crowds. With a 30-foot radius you can hit a huge number of targets, and with a 10-minute duration you can easily kill anything stuck in the area for an extended period.
    
    The 4d10 damage is fine, and negating invisibility is great, but the real appeal is the levels of Exhaustion. The penalties on d20 tests and to speed make it progressibley easier to keep enemies in the area, and pushing enemies back into the area will get progressively easier the more times you do it. Enemies will either die from gradual damage or from hitting 6 levels of Exhaustion as the stacking penalties to their d20 tests make the save functionally impossible to pass.
    
-   Spellfire Storm ([FRHoF](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): The damage isn’t great and Constitution saves aren’t reliable, but the AOE is decently large and this can prevent enemies from casting spells. The Con save when casting a spell is identical to Counterspell, so you’re effectively countering every spell that enemies try to cast while in the area. I would only use when facing enemy spellcasters (the damage isn’t good enough otherwise), but with enemy spellcasters on the field this can easily define an encounter.
    
    We can also compare Spellfire Storm to Moonbeam, which also creates a column of Radiant damage. Spellfire Storm deals the same damage as Moonbeam cast at the same spell level and uses the same save, but instead of bother Shapeshifters, Spellfire Storm shuts down enemy spellcasters. Spellfire Storm can’t be moved, but the considerably larger radius means that you don’t need to move it in many encounters. In close quarters, enemies may be unable to move out of the area. Of course, that also means that you can’t use forced movement to repeatedly trigger the spell’s damage like you could with area effects which don’t cover the entire battlefield.
    
    Casting as a Circle Spell: 25,000 gp to do this is absolutely insane. This is something that should happen as a plot point, not as a player tactic. Maybe you drop this on an extraplanar gate so that your party can fight waves of demons to shut down the gate or something similarly epic and plot-defining.
    
-   Spirit of Death ([BoMT](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Summon Undead is arguably better, but this is still a good summon.
    
    For more help, see our [Practical Guide to Summoning Spells](https://rpgbot.net/dnd5/characters/summoning#spirit-of-death).
    
-   Stone Shape ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Fantastic utility, but probably not worth your only level 4 spell slot each day.
-   Stoneskin ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): An excellent defensive buff.
-   Storm Sphere ([EEPC / XGtE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Passable area control for an extended fight, but the damage is poor, and 1 minute of this probably isn’t enough for your single level 4 spell slot. Sickening Radiance is much better.
-   Summon Aberration ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Three diverse and effective combat options.
    
    For more help, see our [Practical Guide to Summoning Spells](https://rpgbot.net/dnd5/characters/summoning#summon-aberration).
    
-   Summon Construct ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Not as effective offensively as Summon Aberration, but all three summoned constructs are unusually durable for creatures summoned by similar spells.
    
    For more help, see our [Practical Guide to Summoning Spells](https://rpgbot.net/dnd5/characters/summoning#summon-construct).
    
-   Summon Elemental ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Summon Aberration is better offensively, and Summon Construct provides a more durable summon. Summon Elemental’s biggest advantage is that you can summon a creature that can fight effectively underwater, but even then it’s not a great summon.
    
    For more help, see our [Practical Guide to Summoning Spells](https://rpgbot.net/dnd5/characters/summoning#summon-elemental).
    
-   Vitriolic Sphere ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Just upcast Fireball.
-   Wall of Fire ([PHB](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): A great area control option.
-   Vitriolic Sphere ([EEPC / XGtE](//rpgbot.net/dnd5/abbreviations/)): Weak damage. Cast Fireball.
