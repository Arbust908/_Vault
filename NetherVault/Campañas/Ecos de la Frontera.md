> The Keep sits at the edge of the map — past the last road, past the last town, past the last person who knew what they were doing out here. People come to the Borderlands for all kinds of reasons. None of them good. But a century ago something fell from the sky and buried itself in the hills nearby, and last week the earth cracked open and the monsters started moving. Whatever came down in that meteor has had a hundred years to get comfortable. And it has been very busy.
> 
> The Caves of Carnage didn't always exist. A hundred years ago a meteor struck the hills east of the Keep, carving out a labyrinth of tunnels that monsters quickly made their home. The locals called it cursed land and kept their distance. Then the earthquake came — splitting open a sealed chamber deep in the rock — and something that had been patient for a century began to move. What the meteor carried was not rock and fire. It was a mind. Ancient, alien, without analogue in any bestiary. It found the vermin of the caves and it spoke to them. And they listened. Beneath the chaos of warring monster tribes, an underground temple has been growing in the dark, built by creatures that should not be capable of building anything. The Keep doesn't know what it's dealing with. It has sent for help. That means you.

### Theme

The frontier strips everything down to what matters. A story about what waits at the edge of the known world — and the unsettling discovery that the unknown has been watching back.

### Player buy-in

Characters should have a reason to be this far from anywhere — exile, escape, ambition, or simple restlessness. What matters is that they are curious enough to investigate and stubborn enough not to run when the caves get strange. The Borderlands rewards those who ask questions and punishes those who stop paying attention. The Keep is a refuge, but only for those willing to earn it.
