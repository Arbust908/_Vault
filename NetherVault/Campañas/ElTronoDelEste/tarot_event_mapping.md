# El Trono del Este — Mazo de Eventos × Tarot del Fablemaker

## How It Works at the Table

**Before play:** Separate the deck into three piles:
1. **Coins** (Ace through King) → Fortuna (good)
2. **Swords** (Ace through King) → Adversidad (bad)
3. **Major Arcana** (0–XXI) → Lo Extraño (weird)

Set aside **Cups** and **Wands** — these are your expansion deck for mid-to-late campaign.

**Each conquest turn:** Shuffle all three piles together into one deck. Draw based on settlement size:

| Settlement Size | Buildings | Cards Drawn |
|----------------|-----------|-------------|
| **Campamento** | 1–5 | 1 |
| **Aldea** | 6–12 | 2 |
| **Pueblo** | 13–20 | 3 |
| **Ciudad** | 21+ | 4 |

The **suit** tells you the category. The **specific card** tells you which event. Resolve, set aside. When the deck runs low or the settlement reaches a new size tier, reshuffle.

## 🏺 CUPS + 🪄 WANDS — Expansion Deck

**28 cards** held in reserve. Introduce them when:
- The settlement reaches **Pueblo** size (13+ buildings)
- The Condesa turns antagonist (new political events)
- Fey contact is established (Feywild-specific events)
- The Troll King's forces escalate (military events)

**Suggested suit assignments:**
- **Cups** → *Fey & Diplomacy events* — emotions, relationships, the otherworldly. Events tied to Aelindra, barbarian clan negotiations, settler morale, and Feywild incursions
- **Wands** → *War & Ambition events* — action, conflict, expansion. Events tied to Gorruk's forces, Condesa's sabotage, military encounters, and territorial disputes

You can write these events as the campaign develops — by then you'll know exactly what tensions exist and what your players care about.

## DM Quick Reference — How to Read a Draw

```
1. Draw a card
2. Check the suit:
   - Coins → Fortuna (good)     → look up the card in the Coins table
   - Swords → Adversidad (bad)  → look up the card in the Swords table
   - Major Arcana → Lo Extraño  → look up the card in the Major Arcana table
   - Cups/Wands → if in play, use expansion events; if not, redraw

3. Read the event name and effect
4. Present it narratively through the Experts
5. Let the players choose a resolution option
6. Set the card aside (don't reshuffle until deck runs low or new size tier)
```

## Optional: The Reversed Rule

If you want to add another layer, use **reversed draws** (card drawn upside down) as a modifier:

- **Reversed Fortuna (Coins):** The good event has a hidden cost or complication. The Caravana arrives, but they're being pursued. The Artesano is skilled, but they're fleeing something.
- **Reversed Adversidad (Swords):** The bad event has a silver lining or is less severe than expected. The Sequía is mild. The Merodeadores are disorganized and easily dealt with.
- **Reversed Lo Extraño (Major Arcana):** The weird event is more ominous, more connected to the deeper campaign threats. The Sueños Compartidos aren't from Aelindra — they're from Gorruk.

This is entirely optional. If it's too much to track, ignore reversals and just use the upright mapping.

## Notes

- **Flexibility is the point.** If a card comes up and the mapped event doesn't feel right for the current moment, swap it for another event from the same suit or improvise based on the card's imagery.
- **The Fablemaker's art does the heavy lifting.** When you draw a card, show it to the players. Let them see the animated art. The event feels more significant when it has a physical, beautiful artifact attached to it.
- **Track which cards have been drawn** on a simple checklist so you know what's left in the deck and can plan accordingly.
