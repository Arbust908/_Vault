## Quest for the Silver Sword | RPG Retro Review — Summary & Highlights

**Video by captcorajus | RPG Retro Reviews** 🎲 _Module:_ Quest for the Silver Sword (TSR, 1992) | _System:_ Basic D&D | _Levels:_ 2–3

---

### 🏰 What Is This Module?

_Quest for the Silver Sword_ is the first adventure in TSR's **Thunder Rift** line — a series of Basic D&D modules set in a self-contained valley designed to be dropped into any campaign. Thunder Rift is canonically part of the **World of Mystara**, likely placed in the Altan Tepes Borderlands of Darokin, though its exact location was intentionally left vague to encourage DMs to make it their own.

The line's distinguishing feature was its **board game aesthetic**: full-color poster-sized battle maps, cardboard standups for PCs, NPCs, and monsters, and a format that made it accessible to complete newcomers to tabletop RPGs.

---

### 📜 The Setup

The adventure hooks the party through a desperate plea from Erik, a young messenger sent by **Burgomaster Gustovan** of the village **Torlynn**. The village has been cursed with an unnatural, persistent winter — despite it being summer — and all previous adventuring parties hired to deal with it have vanished. The PCs are ushered to a ruined keep that once belonged to a **mad wizard named Barrick**, now overrun by wererats led by their cunning leader, **Keshute**.

---

### 🗺️ The Dungeon Room by Room

The keep is compact but well-designed — 16 rooms with multiple paths and meaningful choices at nearly every turn:

|Room|Highlight|
|---|---|
|1 – Cloak Room|Giant rats nesting in old debris|
|2 – Sitting Room|Barrick's skull on a table — lighting its candle summons his ghost, boosting INT for magic-users; smashing it deals 2d4 damage to all|
|3 – Study|An armor golem guards the wizard's spellbook; lawful characters may pass safely|
|4 – Library|A book safe hides a **crystal azure key** — essential for the best treasure later|
|5 – Dining Room|Tarnished silver cutlery worth real coin, plus a **silver bell** that conjures a meal three times daily|
|6 – Tower|The **White Widow** — a unique module monster whose ice-like webs hum like a harp, lulling victims, and whose bite freezes them solid|
|7 – Kitchen|Ratlings caught cooking; combat if the party arrives here before other encounters|
|8 – Tomb|Three zombies and a ghoul; coffins with false bottoms hiding treasure and traps|
|12 – Ballroom|A **gelatinous cube** hiding in refuse, fed by the ratlings|
|13 – Wererat's Chambers|The magic-sealed strongbox — openable only with the crystal key, containing a +1 dagger, wand of magic detection, bag of holding, gems, and 500gp|
|15 – Laboratory|The climactic room: an **ice throne** with the silver sword frozen inside, and Keshute waiting on it|
|16 – Cell|The frozen (but alive) members of the previous adventuring party, including the elf **Elladin** — the sword's true owner|

---

### ⚔️ The Twist Ending

The module's clever narrative turn reveals that it wasn't Barrick who caused the winter — it was actually **the previous adventuring party**. One of them wielded a sword that turned things to ice, and the cunning Keshute exploited Barrick's laboratory to build an **ice throne** that trapped the weapon and plunged the valley into permanent cold.

Freeing Elladin and returning him to the throne causes the silver sword to burst free into his hand — but the throne itself then transforms into an **ice golem**, which must be defeated to finally lift the curse.

The silver sword **cannot be kept** by the PCs — it returns to Elladin regardless. This makes the module's own title something of a misnomer, as captcorajus notes wryly: the PCs aren't really _questing_ for the sword so much as returning it to its rightful owner.

### 🎨 Production & Presentation

- **Cover art** by Jeff Easley — competent but depicts a scene not found anywhere in the module
- **Poster battlemat** by Robin Raab — the clear highlight of the package, well-rendered and functional for VTT use
- **Interior art** by Karl Waller — described as functional but uninspired compared to earlier TSR works
- The module is only **16 pages**, making it well-suited for home printing
- Available as a PDF on **DriveThruRPG for $4.99**, or for Fantasy Grounds on Steam for $9.99; physical copies run $30–$100 on eBay depending on condition and completeness

### 💡 Key Takeaways

- An excellent **introductory module** for young or new players, designed to follow naturally from _Zanzer's Dungeon_ in the Basic D&D boxed set
- Rewards **exploration and curiosity** (the crystal key, the silver bell) while punishing hack-and-slash approaches
- The dungeon layout, while small, offers multiple routes — a hallmark of solid dungeon design
- Despite its age and modest scope, it holds up as a **fun one-shot** for conventions, kids' events, or anyone wanting a taste of early 90s D&D design philosophy
