## 3. Tone & Style  

**Narrative Lens**  
- **Third‑person limited, dual POV.** Chapters alternate between Elias’s earnest, metaphor‑laden thoughts (“hope fluttered like a skylark in his ribs”) and Rowena’s precise, almost surgical observations.  
- Periodically allow a **one‑line omniscient flourish**—a village proverb or coven maxim—that feels like folklore annotating the tale.

**Mood Palette**  
- *Pastoral sunlight vs. gothic shadow:* golden wheat fields explode against gray stone cottages and candlelit back rooms.  
- **Sensory saturation:** Describe smells first (lanolin, wood‑smoke, crushed thyme), use color as emotional shorthand (Rowena’s violet‑stained fingers signal danger or intimacy).

**Dialogue Cadence**  
- **Elias:** colloquial, earnest, occasionally poetic without knowing it. Short sentences when nervous.  
- **Rowena:** formal syntax laced with sly wordplay; she turns idioms on their heads to test listeners.

**Humor & Heart**  
- Lean on situational comedy—Elias mistaking a rare herb for a weed, Rowena arching an eyebrow so high villagers swear it touches her hairline.  
- Romance simmers, never gushes; tension lives in held breath, shared tools, matching scars.

**Magic Aesthetic**  
- Glyph‑craft reads like botanical calculus: circles of yarrow ash, algebraic curves of wormwood ink.  
- Spells *sound* tactile—soft fizz, iron pop, wind that exhales syllables older than the valley.

**Pacing**  
- **Scene‑driven** with crisp transitions: each chapter begins with a concrete action (mending, measuring, mixing) and ends on a question or reveal.  
- Keep paragraphs short when danger escalates; stretch sentences into rolling cadences when describing landscapes or emotions swelling.

Use this blend of earthy realism and arcane lyricism to keep readers smelling the loam underfoot while glancing nervously at the stars above.
