# Summary: The Three Faction Rule for D&D Political Campaigns

**Source:** Mystic Arts (Di) — Professional writer, director, and 10+ year DM

---

## Core Idea

Creating compelling political intrigue in D&D is surprisingly simple using a repeatable formula: **the Three Faction Rule**. Every political situation is built from three factions — **Good, Bad, and Ugly** — then made complex by nesting, layering, and fracturing those factions until the structure is unrecognizable to players.

## The Six-Step Process

1. **Conflict** — Start with a simple premise (e.g., hobgoblins are attacking civilization).
2. **Factions** — Establish three factions: one aligned with the players (Good), one opposing (Bad), and one neutral wildcard (Ugly). The third faction should be the tipping point that determines the outcome.
3. **Characters** — Populate each faction with memorable NPCs. The "good" faction should have flawed or incompetent leadership so the players feel essential. Factions are perceived through their NPCs.
4. **Ideology** — Give each faction a simple worldview that explains their motivations and makes conflict between them feel natural.
5. **Methods** — Define how each faction operates (diplomacy, military force, dark rituals, etc.) to make interactions feel distinct.
6. **Twist** — Nest sub-factions within each faction using the same Good/Bad/Ugly lens. This creates fractal layers of complexity.

## Key Design Principles

- The **player's side should be struggling** — if factions are evenly matched, there's no adventure.
- The **neutral faction is the kingmaker** — whoever secures them likely wins the conflict.
- **Players interact with NPCs, not factions** — so character design is critical.
- **Fractal depth**: every faction can contain its own three sub-factions, and those can contain sub-factions of their own, creating rich webs of allegiance and tension.

## Variations & Advanced Techniques

- Make all factions morally bad, but place a good sub-faction inside each as a source of hope.
- Make all factions "ugly" (morally grey) with good and bad actors within each.
- Allow players to eliminate factions, creating power vacuums that spawn new ones.
- Add or remove factions over the course of a campaign to keep things dynamic.

## Running Example

- **Duchy of Riverfell** (Good) — A constitutional monarchy led by an aging, ineffective Duke with a capable but powerless daughter.
- **The Pale Fist** (Bad) — A hobgoblin military autocracy led by the brilliant General Varsuk Khal.
- **The Eldritch Order** (Ugly) — A secretive wizard society that worships an enigmatic god and could ally with either side.

Within Riverfell alone: the military wants to fight but is outmatched, the advisors think there's no real threat, and the Duke is too old to reconcile them — creating a three-faction dynamic inside the "good" faction itself.
