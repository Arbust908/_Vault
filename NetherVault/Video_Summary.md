# Video Summary: Fractal World-Building for TTRPGs

**Channel:** Mystic Arts | **Creator:** Donnie (professional writer, director, 10+ year DM)

---

## Core Thesis

You can build a massive, living campaign world by treating details like **fractals** — each area is an "instanced world" that contains sub-areas, which are themselves instanced worlds, going infinitely deeper. This mirrors how the real world feels: no matter where you look, there are always more details to discover.

## Key Definitions

- **Campaign → Adventure → Session:** One campaign contains many adventures; one adventure contains many sessions.
- **World → Setting → Location:** One world contains many settings; one setting contains many locations. A world and a setting are *not* the same thing.

## The Method: Fractal Instance Worlds

1. **Start broad with clichés.** Reduce unfamiliar areas to simple genre shorthand ("cowboy land," "pirate land," "samurai land") when you're not actively playing there.
2. **Render detail on arrival.** The moment players visit an area, reveal that the simple label hides a complex, layered setting with its own sub-cultures, factions, histories, and conflicts.
3. **Keep zooming in.** Each sub-area contains further sub-areas. A "cowboy continent" contains a southern frontier, a northern wilderness, an industrial metropolis, a sewer society, revolutionary factions, cults — each one another fractal layer.
4. **Build through play.** Every adventure permanently changes and defines something about the world. You expand the world one adventure at a time, not by pre-building everything.

## Why Mix Genres?

- The real world mixes genres constantly (samurai overlapping with the telegraph era, guns in China before the Norman Conquest, the Zulu vs. the British Empire).
- Genre contrast creates **verisimilitude** — the feeling that the world has more depth than any single theme.
- Favorite fiction does this too: a gardener in a heroic fellowship, a funny rock man in a mythological battle, a beat cop in an action thriller.

## Practical Advice

- **Don't hem yourself in.** If a new supplement or adventure doesn't fit one setting, find or create another setting within your world where it does.
- **Steal settings, not just adventures.** Run published modules in your world by adjusting names and context. Your world grows with every adventure you absorb.
- **Embrace the Dunning-Kruger effect of world-building.** The more you create, the more questions emerge. That's the point — stop trying to "finish" and enjoy the creative hamster wheel.
- **Subvert expectations through depth.** Players arrive expecting a cliché and discover sub-cultures, new gods, forgotten pantheons, and layered histories.

## The Payoff

The greatest verisimilitude is the feeling that there is always more to know than anyone could ever fully understand — just like the real world. Over time, the DM actually *does* know an enormous amount about their world, and it becomes genuinely real to the table.

## Notable Examples from the Video

- **Orathon** — Donnie's mortal plane, containing cowboys, knights, pirates, Vikings, pharaohs, dwarves, and an industrialized Victorian-style city called Blackard.
- **Ariston** — The "cowboy continent" that contains a Magnificent 7-style south, a Hateful 8-style north, and the industrial metropolis of Blackard.
- **The Ratigan** — A sewer-dwelling society with revolutionaries, regular citizens, and a faction plotting world domination with a cult that wants to raise a serpent to eat the sun.
