**Concise Summary**
Seth Skorkowsky revisits the 1981 AD\&D module *The Secret of Bone Hill*, praising its remarkably dense 32-page sandbox setting centered on the coastal village of Restenford and the eponymous ruined fortress. He highlights the wealth of NPCs, rumors, and modular adventure sites that encourage player-driven exploration, while noting the need for modern DMs to convert encounters for 5E and to clean up the original maps. His overall verdict is that it’s a brilliantly ambitious, high-potential classic—one that rewards diligent prep and creative supplementation.

**Pros**

* **Rich Sandbox Setting**: Packs a huge overland hex map, village, multiple distinct adventure sites and dungeons into just 32 pages.
* **Vibrant NPC Cast**: 315 named residents (55 stat-ted NPCs) provide deep role-playing hooks and personal favorites for each PC.
* **Rumor System**: Over 35 rumors—true and false—plus mechanics for leveled NPCs to debunk myths, driving investigation.
* **Player Agency**: Completely non-linear; parties can tackle locations in any order, build a stronghold, join factions, or invent their own goals.
* **Moral Complexity**: Monster families (including children) force tough choices, inspiring memorable “are we the baddies?” moments.

**Cons**

* **Illegible Maps**: Black-and-white hex and village maps are hard to read, requiring DM redrawing or Photoshop cleanup.
* **Sparse NPC Detail**: While numerically impressive, many NPCs lack distinct personalities or motivations.
* **Zero-Level Rumor Quirk**: Rules say un-leveled locals know no rumors, which feels unrealistic and hampers early clues.
* **No Overarching Plot**: Offers locations but no clear “why you’re here” or central hook—can overwhelm newer DMs.
* **Edition Conversion Work**: Monsters, spells, and treasure need more than stat adjustments to preserve intended challenge.

**Homebrew Takeaways**

* **Player-Filled Maps**: Hand out blank versions of your setting map and let players chart their exploits as they explore.
* **Secret Thieves’ Marks**: Seed faded symbols on docks or ruins to guide perceptive rogues toward hidden doors.
* **Terrain-Specific Encounters**: Craft unique random-encounter tables for forests, riversides, highways and mountains.
* **Monster Family Dilemmas**: Stat up children and elderly among evil lairs to create gut-wrenching moral choices.
* **Rumor-Debunk Mechanic**: Give higher-level NPCs a chance to correct falsehoods, encouraging smart clue-gathering.
* **Layered Side Quests**: Stub in modular hooks (e.g. Hydra lair, witches with magical mazes, dwarven mine recoveries) tied to cover-art illusions.
* **Sandbox Intro Hook**: Start PCs as barge guards or new hires to justify arrival, then offer multiple faction invitations (garrison, abbey, wizard’s apprentice).
* **Pre-Boss Softening**: Require PCs to cleanse a hidden underwater temple or defeat lesser sites before tackling the big bad fortress.
* **Legacy NPC Threads**: Drop the remains or gear of past heroes (or mentors) to spark investigation and tie in player relationships.
* **Faction-Wide Threats**: Use beacons or signal mirrors between outposts (Orc or Bugbear) to imply a growing enemy network and unify disparate sites.
