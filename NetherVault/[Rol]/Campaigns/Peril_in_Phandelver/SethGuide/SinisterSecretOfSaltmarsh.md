**Concise Summary**
The reviewer gives “The Sinister Secret of Saltmarsh” high marks as a low-level, two-part AD\&D module that’s compact (around 30 pages plus fold-out maps) and ideal for first-through-third level characters. They praise its haunted-house hook (despite being a smugglers’ lair in disguise), detailed ship plans for the Sea Ghost sequel, and plentiful artwork to aid storytelling. However, they call out the almost non-existent village background and lack of an in-module motivation for why PCs would investigate the house, recommending that GMs flesh in town details and plot hooks. Overall, it’s deemed a classic that can be run in just two or three sessions (plus one more for the ship adventure), with plenty of room for a clever DM to expand and personalize.

---

**Pros**

* **Perfect for low-level parties**: Designed for levels 1–3, rare among modules of the era.
* **Two-part structure**: Seamlessly shifts from a “haunted house” mystery to a ship-board stealth/combat sequel.
* **Strong visual support**: Fold-out maps and abundant artwork aid both novice and veteran DMs.
* **Pacing**: Compact enough for 2–3 sessions (plus one more for the Sea Ghost), avoiding marathon campaigns.
* **Replay value**: Flexible hooks and room for GM customization keep it fresh over multiple runs.
* **Rewarding encounters**: From giant spiders and illusionist mooks to pseudo-dragon pets and aquatic elf allies.

---

**Cons**

* **Town underdevelopment**: Saltmarsh itself is barely sketched—DMs must build NPCs, locations, and local color from scratch.
* **Missing adventure hook**: No in-text rationale for why PCs head to the “haunted” house; requires GM invention.
* **Tone shift risks**: Moving from spooky investigations to straightforward combat on the ship can feel abrupt.
* **Short sequel**: Part 2 (the Sea Ghost) plays very quickly—some groups may find it too brief or simplistic.
* **Reliance on DM prep**: Both town and motivations hinge entirely on homebrewed material or GM handouts.

---

**Homebrew Takeaways**

* **Shipwreck or escort hook**: Drop PCs into a new locale via a storm, cargo protection gig, or competitive hero wager—guaranteed motivation.
* **Tavern-rumor setup**: Use an NPC story in the tavern to organically incite investigation (and give free drinks as XP!).
* **Fold-out maps & handouts**: Invest in well-illustrated, foldable maps (haunted house, ship deck) and in-world parchment for clues.
* **NPC twist**: Introduce a seemingly rescued ally who’s secretly an assassin or double agent—keeps players guessing.
* **Environmental foreshadowing**: Seed rumors of lizard-folk skirmishes or marshland omens early for narrative payoff.
* **Modular two-act design**: Build a “false haunting” mystery that transitions into a field-mission sequel (e.g., ship raid, fortress assault).
* **Magical creature allies**: Include chained or enslaved beings (aquatic elf, pseudo-dragon) that can bond with PCs if treated well.
* **Illusionist boss fight**: Leverage low-level illusions or undead guardians to surprise players accustomed to direct combat.
