**Concise Summary**
Tomb of the Lizard King is praised for its crisp PDF and Print-on-Demand quality and its pioneering twist—an undead, spell-casting vampire lizard monarch—but lambasted as a brutal, meat-grinder tournament module that feels shoe-horned into a campaign. The reviewer applauds the intrigue of a mid-session assassination attempt, varied overland encounters, and the sheer audacity of a vampire-mage boss, yet criticizes its lack of foreshadowing, over-reliance on save-or-die traps, and the artificial capping of cleric power. Ultimately, it’s hailed as an “idea mine” rather than a runnable adventure: steal the gems, ditch the execution.

**Pros**

* **High-quality reprints**: The bookmarked, high-resolution PDF and fully usable Print-on-Demand book (no missing fold-outs) earn full thumbs-up.
* **Memorable opening**: A surprise assassination attempt with Flame Strike sets a gripping tone.
* **Pioneering villain twist**: Introducing a vampire-king monster in AD\&D was groundbreaking.
* **Varied overland structure**: Multiple thematic encounters (refugees, clerics, a crystal-ball-observed journey) enrich the trek.
* **Modular design**: Distinct sections—intrigue, overland, dungeon—make it easy to mine ideas.

**Cons**

* **Meat-grinder difficulty**: Encounters (20 lizardmen at a bridge, a 400-year-old black dragon, 16 wights, shambling mounds, demons) and save-or-die traps overwhelm intermediate-level parties.
* **Claustrophobic cleric cap**: Forbidding clerics above 7th level to preserve the ending neuters player choice and inflates party size.
* **Sparse foreshadowing**: Sakatha’s return comes as a jarring reveal rather than a built-up climax.
* **Tournament-style feel**: Pre-gens, brutal odds, and lethal slides convey score-keeping more than storytelling.
* **Design inconsistencies**: AI oversights (lizard men ignoring a 16-wight battle), unrealistically slow swamp travel, and unscoped illusion-and-trap mechanics.

**Homebrew Takeaways**

* **Assassination kick-off**: Begin with an unexpected attack on a patron NPC to hook players immediately.
* **Rival parties on exhibit**: Stage an “adventurer audition” courtyard so PCs compete with—and spy on—other groups.
* **Reactive villain via scrying**: Let the big bad monitor the party with a crystal ball and alter plans dynamically.
* **Mythic rumor network**: Scatter true and false whispers early to build tension before a reveal.
* **Environmental navigation clues**: Use smoke, heat, painted skulls, or broken camps to guide (or mislead) players.
* **Lyric-sheet handouts**: Provide printable songs or poems for NPC minstrels to deepen immersion.
* **NPC-driven timers**: Introduce a charmed envoy who races to alert the patron, creating a real-time countdown.
* **Prize beyond gold**: Offer land grants, marriages, or titles instead of mere coin to motivate high-level parties.
* **Adaptive monster stats**: When porting across editions, rebalance creatures’ power rather than converting mechanically.
