# Resume Review Insights

## Key Learnings

- Lead with a clear professional position: Senior Full Stack Engineer with a frontend specialization in React, Vue, and TypeScript.
- Place technical skills near the beginning so recruiters and ATS filters can identify relevant keywords quickly.
- Use exact technology names instead of relying on broad descriptions or implied experience.
- Prioritize outcomes over responsibilities by including user scale, team size, transaction volume, uptime, performance, accessibility, and conversion metrics.
- Present technical leadership without obscuring hands-on engineering work. Relevant evidence includes building teams, leading migrations, establishing design systems, and using analytics to guide product decisions.
- Make language proficiency and international remote experience explicit when they are relevant hiring filters.
- Adapt each language version to its target market instead of translating it literally.
- Preserve widely recognized industry terms when they improve clarity or ATS matching, including Frontend, Full Stack, Design System, stack, and Product Owner.
- Treat Senior Frontend Engineer, Senior Full Stack Engineer, and Tech Lead as aligned target roles.
- Consider Engineering Manager roles only when the resume can demonstrate people management, hiring, feedback, performance reviews, and planning responsibilities.
- Treat market research as time-sensitive. Revalidate role demand, links, salary information, and requested tools before each application campaign.
- Keep the existing resume as the source of truth when an external review introduces conflicting dates, metrics, or technologies.
- Record unresolved claims as follow-up questions rather than silently choosing one version.
- Keep separate resume files for each language so one version can evolve without creating regressions in the other.
- Check pagination after moving sections. Adding skills near the top can turn a two-page resume into three pages when forced page breaks remain.

## DO

- DO use a concise summary that communicates seniority, specialization, scope, and product orientation.
- DO include the exact keywords used in relevant job descriptions when they accurately reflect real experience.
- DO connect technologies to concrete work and measurable results.
- DO quantify accomplishments with verified numbers whenever possible.
- DO distinguish concurrent contracts from sequential full-time roles.
- DO show remote collaboration with international teams and stakeholders.
- DO include English proficiency when it is confirmed.
- DO keep contact information easy to find near the top of the document.
- DO retain credible evidence of both individual contribution and technical leadership.
- DO use consistent dates, job titles, punctuation, terminology, and number formatting within each language version.
- DO verify every metric and date against the current source resume before publishing.
- DO flag missing evidence, such as migration improvements, design-system impact, A/B test results, and people-management scope.
- DO review the rendered PDF for page count, spacing, wrapping, and visual hierarchy.
- DO validate syntax and formatting before considering a revision complete.

## DON'T

- DON'T add any marketable tool without demonstrable experience.
- DON'T inflate user counts, performance gains, leadership scope, or business impact.
- DON'T overwrite verified information with conflicting claims from an external review.
- DON'T bury core technologies inside long experience paragraphs.
- DON'T describe only duties when outcomes or scale are available.
- DON'T claim Engineering Manager positioning based solely on technical leadership.
- DON'T translate technical terminology mechanically when the established English term is clearer to recruiters.
- DON'T duplicate every keyword in every section solely for ATS optimization.
- DON'T let the skills section become an unprioritized inventory of every technology ever used.
- DON'T introduce unsupported market claims or stale job links into the resume itself.
- DON'T preserve forced page breaks without checking how new content affects pagination.
- DON'T modify the English resume while creating the Spanish version unless both versions require the same verified correction.
- DON'T consider a source-only review sufficient; inspect the generated PDF whenever the rendering tool is available.
