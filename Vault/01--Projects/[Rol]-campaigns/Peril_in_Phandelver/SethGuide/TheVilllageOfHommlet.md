**Concise Summary**
The reviewer acknowledges *The Village of Hommlet* as a pioneering, village-focused AD\&D module that left a lasting nostalgic mark, particularly due to its evocative maps and unique NPCs like Burne and Rufus. However, they argue that the original T1 release feels incomplete and DM-unfriendly, with sparse naming, scattered key information, and zero in-town adventure hooks. Much of its impact hinges on a sequel that arrived six years later, leaving a standalone village adventure that often stalls novice parties. The overall verdict: historically important, but requiring substantial DM prep or better—and more self-contained—first-edition alternatives.

---

**Pros**

* **Trailblazing Village Focus:** One of the first modules to center gameplay around an NPC-populated village rather than a dungeon.
* **Evocative, Reusable Maps:** Clear layouts of buildings and the Moathouse dungeon, easily reskinned or recolored for modern tables.
* **Rich, “Photo-Bombing” NPCs:** Iconic characters (e.g. Burne & Rufus) lend personality and inspiration for memorable encounters.
* **Attention to Flavor Detail:** Price lists, dish descriptions, building appearances—great fodder for atmospheric role-play.
* **Seedbed for Homebrew Ideas:** Plenty of half-formed concepts (rat infestations, brigand lairs, cult spies) to steal and expand.

---

**Cons**

* **Inconsistent NPC Naming:** Most villagers left unnamed, forcing DMs to invent dozens of names on the fly.
* **Scattered Key Information:** Important notes (e.g. bogus holy water, Temple background) buried in non-intuitive sections.
* **Zero In-Village Hooks:** No rumors, quests, or leads in town to drive characters toward the Moathouse.
* **Sequel-Dependent Structure:** The module teases the Temple of Elemental Evil heavily but doesn’t supply the necessary lore until a later release—six years after.
* **Uneven Challenge Spikes:** Fifth-level cleric Lareth and a potential tenth-level assassin ambush threaten novice PCs with unfair difficulty.
* **Not DM-Friendly for Beginners:** Lacks ready-to-go keys, area labels, or guidance, demanding significant prep from novice GMs.

---

**Homebrew Takeaways**

* **Pre-Name Your NPCs:** Compile a list of village names (key and incidental) in advance—post-it them behind your screen for instant recall.
* **Create a Quick-Reference Key:** Map area numbers to short labels (e.g. “7 – Inn,” “13 – Trading Post”) for fast table flips during play.
* **Kick Off with Purposeful Encounters:** Have PCs escort a caravan or repel bandits en route to town to bond the party and introduce their first NPC.
* **Weave in Local Hooks:** Scatter livestock raids, goblin sightings or missing persons to organically lead PCs toward the Moathouse.
* **Reimagine Brigands as Cult Initiates:** Dress highwaymen in incomplete cult regalia, add sacrificial altars or tithing notes to hint at deeper evil.
* **Layer in Traps & Rewards:** Fortify back-door entries with snares or bear traps, and offer bounties or special recognition for clearing brigand or cult threats.
* **Leverage Color Art & Handouts:** Source or create vivid, full-color maps and handouts to replace the original black-and-white schematics.
