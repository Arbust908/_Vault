**The Tale of the Owlbears: A Warning from the Whispering Woods**

Once, long ago, when the moon hung low and the wind carried secrets through the trees, there lived a creature so terrible that even the bravest hunters would not speak its name aloud. The elders called it the *Owlbear*—a beast born of shadow and hunger, with the body of a bear and the face of an owl. Its eyes glowed like embers in the dark, and its claws could split stone as easily as flesh.

This is the story of little Liora, who did not heed the warnings of the village elders. And it is a tale you must remember, children, lest the same fate befall you.

---

### **The Girl Who Wandered Too Far**

Liora was a curious child, always chasing fireflies into the tall grass or climbing trees to see what lay beyond the village walls. Her mother would scold her, saying, *"The forest is not for children, Liora. It is for the Owlbears."*

But Liora laughed. *"Owlbears are just stories,"* she said. *"I’ve never seen one, and neither have you!"*

One evening, as the sun bled into twilight, Liora slipped away from the village. She wanted to pick the sweetest blackberries that grew deep in the woods, where no one else dared to go. The trees loomed tall, their branches twisting like skeletal fingers, and the air smelled of damp earth and something… *wrong*.

*"Just a little farther,"* Liora whispered to herself.

Then she heard it.

A sound like bones cracking in the dark.

*Snap. Snap. Snap.*

Liora froze. The berries fell from her hands.

From the shadows between the trees, two golden eyes opened—huge, unblinking, and *hungry*. A low growl rumbled through the undergrowth, shaking the leaves from the branches.

The Owlbear stepped forward.

It was twice the size of a man, its feathers ruffled like a storm cloud, its claws digging into the soil. Its beak clicked open, and a sound like a dying man’s whisper slithered out:

*"Little one… you should not be here."*

Liora’s breath came in ragged gasps. She turned to run—but the Owlbear was faster.

---

### **The Hunt**

The beast moved like a shadow, silent as death. Liora sprinted through the trees, her heart pounding in her ears. Branches whipped at her face, roots tangled her feet, but she did not dare look back.

Behind her, the Owlbear’s laughter echoed—a sound like breaking glass.

*"Run, little mouse,"* it hissed. *"Run, run, run."*

Liora’s lungs burned. Her legs trembled. She could see the edge of the forest now, the warm glow of the village lanterns just beyond the trees.

*Almost there.*

Then—

A searing pain shot through her ankle. She screamed as she fell, rolling onto her back just in time to see the Owlbear looming over her, its beak dripping with saliva.

*"No one escapes the Whispering Woods,"* it crooned.

Liora squeezed her eyes shut, waiting for the end.

But then—

A loud *CRACK* split the air.

The Owlbear shrieked, its body jerking as if struck by lightning. Liora opened her eyes to see an old woman standing between her and the beast, her hands raised high. It was *Grandmother Hilda*, the village wise woman, her silver hair glowing in the moonlight.

*"Back, demon!"* Hilda commanded. *"This child is under my protection!"*

The Owlbear snarled, its feathers bristling. For a moment, it seemed as if it would attack—but then, with a final, bone-chilling screech, it melted back into the darkness.

Hilda pulled Liora to her feet. *"Foolish girl,"* she muttered. *"Did you think the stories were just tales?"*

Liora shook her head, tears streaming down her face. *"I—I didn’t believe—"*

*"Believe now,"* Hilda said grimly. *"The Owlbears are real. And they are always watching."*

---

### **The Lesson of the Owlbears**

Liora returned to the village that night, her clothes torn, her soul shaken. She never spoke of what happened in the woods—not to her friends, not even to her mother. But from that day on, she never wandered past the village borders again.

And neither should *you*, children.

For the Owlbears are still out there.

They wait in the deepest parts of the forest, where the trees grow too close and the moonlight never touches the ground. They listen for the laughter of careless children, for the crunch of leaves under small, unguarded feet.

And when they hear you…

Oh, when they hear you…

They *come.*

---

### **The Warning**

So remember this, little ones, when the fire burns low and the wind howls at your window:

- **Never go into the woods alone.**
- **Never stay out past dusk.**
- **And never, ever doubt the tales of the Owlbears.**

For the forest is not your friend.

And the Owlbears?

They are *always hungry.*

**The End.**
