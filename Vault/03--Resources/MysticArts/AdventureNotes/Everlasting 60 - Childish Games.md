## Before We Begin
Sometimes the best feedback you can get is when you recognize yourself in others. I played a game online yesterday and there were some flaws in that game I recognized in the games I run and I wanted to fix them. Basically, I want to try and land **scenes** better.

* I want to do a better job **echoing** your requests.
* I want you guys to help me by **creating scenes** when you start talking.
* I want to be a little more active at policing the roleplay to facilitate immersion.

And on top of that all, I want to make sure we avoid one thing I notice in myself and others. There's been a lot of us being a little short with one another at the table, sometimes in tone or otherwise dismissive.

For me, this tends to make me kind of "hang up" and stop talking. Which I assume some others might experience on occasion. So, let's try and police our tone a little bit more. If only for my autism.

#### The Table Rules
**Flow Comes First.** Let's play a game where things get done.
* We'll keep rule arguments to a minimum, stick to rulings where possible.
* If you have a hard time with focus, try keeping notes.
* Have your dice out, your character sheet available.

**Immersion is King.** Let's play a game where people are immersed.
* Respect the scene: keep meta talk to a minimum.
* Respect the characters: don't joke when people are roleplaying.
* Respect the ignorance: artless exposition might be worse than focused ignorance.
* Respect the player: players can ask for what they need, don't volunteer it unprompted.
